# Otium 0.1.5

- **Projected earnings per day, week, month and year.** The earnings card now starts with what your current pace
  would bring each day, week, month and year, after Otium's fee and electricity, and counts up to the new numbers when
  they change. It sits above a separate row of what you actually earned today, this week, this month and this year,
  so projected and real amounts can't be mixed up. Hidden while mining doesn't pay at your electricity price.
- **A chart for the day, week, month and year.** Pick a range above the chart: the last 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days or
  12 months. The bars have labels, grow in one after another, and pointing at one shows what that hour, day or month
  earned. Otium now keeps daily totals for over a year to draw them (older history fills in from the hours it still had).
- **Amounts in your own currency.** Earnings, balances and projections show in the currency of your computer's region
  (euros as "1.234,56 €", pounds, kroner and so on), at the day's exchange rate, in the number style of your locale.
  Settings has a Currency card to pick another one. Everything is still earned and worked out in dollars, so cash-outs
  show both ("$5.00 (≈ €4.60)"), and the electricity price stays in dollars per kWh. Rates come from Otium's server
  (twice a day, no account data in the request) and are kept on your computer, so amounts still convert offline.
  Without rates yet, amounts show in dollars.
- **Smoother screens.** Moving between the Overview and Advanced slides a short way and fades, from the right when you
  go deeper and from the left when you go back. Otium keeps the screen you leave, so going back shows the Overview as it was
  instead of building it again and letting everything pop in a second time. The earned and projected amounts count up. Switching the chart between
  day, week, month and year morphs the bars into the new ones instead of redrawing them, and the Day / Week / Month /
  Year choice fades between states. A soft pulse beside "Earned so far" shows while Otium is earning.
- **A new look: the night shift.** The app now has the website's look: a deep blue-green night behind everything, see-through
  cards, mono labels and an amber accent. The night is alive: emerald and blue glows wander across it, two soft aurora bands
  slide and breathe, stars twinkle and creep sideways, small lights rise and sway, and now and then a shooting star crosses.
  It all speeds up and brightens while Otium is earning, so you can tell from across the room, and a soft sweep of light
  crosses the balance card. It stops moving, and costs nothing, while the window is hidden or minimized. It is always dark,
  like the website.
- **A home screen that fits, and never scrolls.** The Overview is two columns that fill the window: balance, what is running
  and the invite on the left, your earnings on the right with the chart taking the height that is left, and one suggestion
  at a time under it. Nothing on it expands, so it never grows into a scrollbar. What needs more room opens as a sheet over the
  screen instead: a device's readings (click its row in "Right now"), cash-out with your earlier cash-outs beside it, choosing
  internet-sharing networks, and a result to share (paid, while you were away, last month, the test). Notices (update ready,
  antivirus, electricity pause, the test running) are one slim line under the header. The balance and its projected month no
  longer overlap, and long lines are shortened with the full text in a tooltip.
- **One fixed window size.** The window is 1100 × 780 and can't be resized or maximized, so it never stretches over a
  big screen with a narrow strip in the middle. The Overview uses two columns (balance, results and what is running on
  the left, your earnings and tips on the right). On a screen too small for that size it shrinks to fit and the page
  switches to one column.
- **Settings in groups.** The cards are grouped under "How Otium works", "Money" and "You".
- **SRBMiner 3.8.3 for graphics cards** (from 3.7.6). Its release notes list Pearl efficiency improvements for NVIDIA
  RTX 3000, 4000 and 5000 cards, a fix for Pearl hashrate and power swings, and Quantus improvements for RTX 3000 to
  5000 cards. The dev fee is unchanged. Graphics cards test their coins again with the new version, as they do after
  any miner update.
- **Live readings and what Otium is doing, in "Right now".** Click the Processor or Graphics card row to see its load,
  temperature, fan, power against its limit, clocks, memory and, when the driver holds the card back, why (too hot, at its
  power limit). A badge on the row says plainly when Otium is **Testing** coins (and which one), **Tuning** a setting,
  **Easing off** because the fans got loud, or **Cooling down**, with a line on what that means for the numbers shown.
- **Overclocking programs step aside.** MSI Afterburner, ASUS GPU Tweak, EVGA Precision, ZOTAC FireStorm, AORUS Engine,
  Gigabyte Xtreme Engine, Sapphire TriXX, Palit ThunderMaster and NVIDIA Inspector are closed when the graphics card
  starts earning and started again when it stops, so your overclock is back for games. They changed fan speeds and
  power limits under the miner: a fan curve that stopped at 90 °C slowed a test to a third on one RTX 3080. Programs
  that run as administrator need Windows' permission once (Settings > Devices). A switch turns this off.
- **Most speed at the fan noise you choose.** Settings has Silent, Quiet, Balanced and Performance (fans at or under
  40, 50, 60 or 75%). While the card earns, Otium raises its power limit step by step as long as the fans stay under
  your limit, lowers it when they get louder, and remembers where it settled for each coin.
- **A hot card is protected, and tests aren't judged on it.** Mining stops at 90 °C, or when a warm, busy card's fan stands
  still, and carries on at 80 °C. A coin's test that ran while the card was throttled by heat is thrown away and repeated
  once it has cooled, so a card that overheated for a few minutes no longer settles on a worse coin. Otium also notices
  when another program undoes its power limit and sets it again.
- **"Optimize my graphics card" is one option.** The fan-noise choice and pausing overclocking programs now sit under it
  in Settings, and work only while it is on.
- **Far less memory while Otium sits in the tray.** With the window closed or minimized Otium now gives back the memory it
  isn't using (about 150 MB down to under 30 MB in Task Manager), and again every ten minutes while it stays hidden. It also
  stops redrawing the background and reading the hardware for the window nobody is looking at, and the garbage collector
  prefers a smaller heap.
- **Projected earnings follow what you're doing now.** A computer that earns whenever it's on, and is mining right now, is
  projected at about 16 hours a day instead of the average of earlier days when it hardly earned.
- **Easier help when a processor or graphics card has a problem.** When one hits an error, the short reason (with file
  paths removed, at most 120 characters) now goes with Otium's regular check-in, so the problem can be understood
  without asking you for a report. The privacy page says so.
