# Idle PCs earn little, so trust must win

As of October 2026, an individual can still sell CPU and GPU time in five ways: GPU rental marketplaces (Vast.ai, Salad, Clore.ai), token-paying decentralized networks (io.net, Nosana, Golem, Theta), crypto mining through pools or hashpower marketplaces (XMRig plus a pool, NiceHash, Kryptex, unMineable), a few AI-inference networks that actually pay (Darkbloom for Apple Silicon, Salad, Hyperbolic), and bandwidth-sharing apps. The pay is thin for almost everyone. A high-end NVIDIA card can earn several dollars a day, but most of that currently comes from one fragile new coin (Pearl). Mainstream CPUs gross $0.25–1.10 a day and lose money at $0.30/kWh. Macs earn cents from mining and about $16 a month from the best paid inference network in an independent test. No incumbent offers one-click setup on Windows, Linux and macOS together. That gap exists because macOS has very little to sell and Linux hosting usually needs a dedicated machine. For a C#/Avalonia cross-platform app, I recommend building five integrations first, in this order: (1) XMRig CPU mining to the user's own wallet via a non-custodial pool, which is the only real-money path on all three OSes; (2) a profit-switching GPU-mining engine for Windows and Linux; (3) a Salad handoff for Windows GPU owners who want AI work and PayPal cash-out; (4) Vast.ai hosting for Linux rigs, which has the richest automation API; and (5) Darkbloom for Apple Silicon. The architecture should be non-custodial throughout, ship a small signed shell that downloads miner engines on demand, and count electricity explicitly. The app's referral program has to be paid for out of a per-user revenue of cents to a few dollars a month. My recommendation is therefore to fund referrals with foregone fees, not cash: single-level, proportional to verified contribution, and never routed through an app-held wallet.

## Only about a dozen paths still pay consumer hardware real money

Since 2025 the market has moved toward data-center supply. RunPod closed Community Cloud to new hosts after expanding Secure Cloud. An aggregator dates this to about February 2026, but the exact closure date is unconfirmed ([RunPod blog](https://www.runpod.io/blog/an-update-on-the-community-cloud-hosting-application-process); [Plushcap](https://www.plushcap.com/companies/runpod/blog/summaries/2026/02)). TensorDock accepts only data-center hosts and was bought by Voltage Park ([TensorDock](https://www.tensordock.com/host.html); [Voltage Park](https://voltagepark.com/blog/tensordock-joins-voltage-park-a-new-chapter-begins)). CUDO closed its public on-demand platform on 31 March 2026, and its consumer Cudo Miner app looks abandoned ([CUDO docs](https://docs.cudocompute.com/); [download.it](https://cudo-miner.en.download.it/)). Most token networks now chase H100/H200 supply and are moving from emissions toward revenue-linked tokenomics. Examples are Akash's Burn-Mint Equilibrium (March 2026) and Nosana's NNP-0001 ([Akash roadmap 2026](https://akash.network/roadmap/2026/); [yellow.com NOS](https://yellow.com/it/asset/nos)). That shift will squeeze subsidy income for low-end consumer hardware. The tables below cover every option the research surfaced, grouped by category, with status, OS, hardware, payout, automation surface, and wrapper/referral/KYC notes.

Salad is the only significant marketplace that runs on an ordinary Windows gaming PC. It runs Linux containers inside a WSL2/Hyper-V VM, needs BIOS virtualization, and does not work behind a VPN. Some game anti-cheat systems refuse to start while virtualization is on ([Salad support](https://support.salad.com/faq/compatibility/do-you-support-macos-and-linux); [Salad community](https://community.salad.com/container-workloads-and-your-pc/); [Salad troubleshooting](https://support.salad.com/article/346-container-workloads-troubleshooting)). Salad says it has "no plans to support MacOS". Its Linux build is an early beta gated by verification and contracts ([Salad Linux onboarding](https://support.salad.com/guides/getting-started/linux-onboarding)). Vast pays in cash, which suits a consumer product: weekly invoices, a $20 minimum, no ACH or wire, and hosts must be able to receive B2B payments ([Vast payment docs](https://docs.vast.ai/host/payment.md)). Hosting, however, means installing Ubuntu, NVIDIA drivers and port forwarding yourself, and restarting during a rental hurts the reliability score ([Vast hosting overview](https://docs.vast.ai/documentation/host/hosting-overview)). Clore's fees steer hosts toward holding its token. On a 100-CLORE listing the host keeps 95 if paid in CLORE but 80 if paid in BTC or USDT ([Clore host fees](https://docs.clore.ai/clore.ai-eng-de/fur-hosts/host-fees)). A consumer app should disclose that token exposure.

io.net is the only crypto network with official install paths for Windows, Linux and Apple Silicon ([io.net quick-start](https://io.net/docs/guides/workers/quick-start-guide); [io.net macOS](https://io.net/docs/guides/workers/install-on-macos.md)). Three things make it a poor first integration. A support article says worker onboarding is disabled ([io.net support](https://support.io.net/en/support/solutions/articles/156000014581-how-to-add-a-new-worker-macos)). Block rewards require a per-device stake whose amount the research could not find. Rewards go to a self-custody Solana wallet, and exchange deposit addresses are explicitly unsupported ([io.net block rewards](https://io.net/docs/guides/block-rewards/block-rewards.md); [io.net wallets](https://io.net/docs/guides/workers/rewards-wallets)). Golem and Nosana are Linux-only. Golem says "x86-64 Linux is the only supported platform" and rejects Docker Desktop on WSL2 ([Golem provider install](https://docs.golem.network/docs/providers/provider-installation)). Nosana is deprecating Windows hosts and runs its node in a privileged container ([Nosana grid-windows](https://docs.nosana.com/hosts/grid-windows); [Nosana host guide](https://learn.nosana.com/hosts/grid-run)). No ROI tracker can measure consumer returns on io.net or Nosana; both are listed as "not measurable yet", and Theta Edge Node is estimated at $0–10/month ([gamefi-roi io.net](https://gamefi-roi-web.onrender.com/opportunities/io-net-worker); [gamefi-roi Theta](https://gamefi-roi-web.onrender.com/opportunities/theta-edge-node)). Several networks require users to buy tokens before they can earn: io.net staking, Flux collateral, Aethir licences, Livepeer LPT. For a consumer app, that turns "sell idle compute" into a financial-product decision.

### Mining and hashpower: the one-click incumbents are all Windows-only

| Option | OS | Payout / minimum | Fees | KYC | Automation & licensing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **XMRig + pool** | **Win, Linux, macOS (incl. ARM), FreeBSD** | Direct to user's XMR wallet; MoneroOcean 0.003 XMR min; P2Pool ~0.00027 XMR, 0% fee, needs own node | 1% XMRig donation + ~1% pool | None at pool | **GPL-3.0; HTTP API** (summary, backends, config, JSON-RPC) |
| **NiceHash** | Consumer apps Windows-only; NHOS on Linux | BTC every 4 h; Lightning payouts from 10 sats | **2% service charge**; withdrawal fees | **Mandatory** | Excavator TCP API (port 3456), REST at api2.nicehash.com; **Excavator EULA bans redistribution** |
| **Kryptex** | Windows app (+Android) | BTC/USDT/ETH etc., gift cards; 0.00025 BTC min; USD via Volet 1.95% | Exchange spread; pool 1–3% | **None advertised** | Closed app; separate pool |
| **unMineable** | unMiner Windows only; stratum works with any miner | 80+ payout coins, per-coin minimums | **1%, or 0.75% with a referral code** | Email only | Stratum endpoint |
| Closed GPU miners (SRBMiner, lolMiner, Rigel) | Windows/Linux; no macOS | Via chosen pool | **0.7–2.5% built-in dev fees** (pearlhash 2%) | — | lolMiner `--apiport`; SRBMiner binaries may run "only as part of an official release" |
| Salad (mining fallback) | Windows | Salad Balance | Undisclosed spread | — | Not controllable |

XMRig is the backbone of any cross-platform mining story. It is GPL-3.0, ships official binaries for all three desktop OSes including ARM64, and exposes an HTTP API that can change configuration and state at runtime ([XMRig GitHub](https://github.com/xmrig/xmrig); [XMRig API](https://xmrig.com/docs/miner/api)). The profitable 2026 GPU algorithms (Pearl, Quantus, ProgPow, KawPow, Autolykos2) are served by closed miners whose dev fees cannot be removed. None of those miners runs on macOS ([SRBMiner](https://github.com/doktor83/SRBMiner-Multi); [lolMiner](https://github.com/Lolliedieb/lolMiner-releases)). NiceHash is the most automatable marketplace, with Excavator built for third-party control ([Excavator](https://github.com/nicehash/excavator)). It now requires KYC for every user, and accounts without it have mining stopped. The date of that change comes from a competitor's article that contradicts itself, but user reports corroborate it ([Kryptex on NiceHash](https://www.kryptex.com/en/articles/nicehash-alternative-en); [Trustpilot](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/www.nicehash.com?page=6)). Kryptex advertises no KYC and gift-card payouts, which makes it the closest comparable product for a "pocket money" app ([kryptex.com](https://www.kryptex.com/en)).

### AI inference and training networks: almost all pay points

Few AI networks pay consumer hardware in real money. **Darkbloom** (Eigen Labs) is the clear exception for Macs. It is a public-alpha network that serves inference on Apple Silicon M1–M4 (macOS 14+, 16 GB+ unified memory). It pays in USD through Stripe Connect within 1–3 business days and takes no platform fee from providers ([The Register, Aug 2026](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/24/conjure-cash-with-old-macs-by-linking-them-to-ai-inference-borg/5292002); [Darkbloom earnings docs](https://docs.darkbloom.dev/provider/earnings.md)). Paid traffic requires MDM enrollment for hardware attestation, re-checked every five minutes ([Darkbloom attestation](https://docs.darkbloom.dev/security/attestation)). Providers drive it through the `darkbloom login/start/status/earnings` CLI. Its terms require a personally owned device and forbid cloud arbitrage ([miyagadget review](https://miyagadget.page/en/blog/2026/06/24/darkbloom-provider-mac-earnings-review-en/)). Salad and Hyperbolic pay real rental income, and Nosana and Bittensor pay tokens. Bittensor is not consumer-viable: mining "is not supported on Windows", registration burns TAO, and the Chutes inference subnet requires Intel TDX confidential-VM GPU nodes ([Learn Bittensor](https://guides.learnbittensor.org/miners); [chutes-miner](https://github.com/rayonlabs/chutes-miner)). Many other networks are paused or pay nothing in cash. Gensyn's RL Swarm is paused ([Gensyn docs](https://docs.gensyn.ai/testnet)). Inference.net's devnet tokens have "no monetary value", and its USDC operator payouts are unconfirmed ([Inference.net devnet](https://docs.devnet.inference.net)). Fortytwo and Dria pay points ([Dria rewards](https://node-guide.dria.co/rewards.md)). Pluralis has no payout mechanism, needs 24 GB VRAM and 80 GB RAM, and requires a North American location ([Pluralis requirements](https://pluralis.ai/docs/quick-start/requirements/)). Psyche screens its contributors ([Nous Research](https://nousresearch.com/?p=2359)). Exo and Petals are volunteer software. Points programs should be valued at zero, because Gensyn shows they can end abruptly once a token launches.

### Bandwidth sharing: real but tiny, with rising enforcement risk

Bandwidth apps pay **$2–25 per device per month**, with cash-out minimums of $2.50 (EarnApp) to $20 (Honeygain, Repocket) ([Proxyway](https://proxyway.com/research/proxyware-passive-income-apps); [Geekflare](https://geekflare.com/finance/bandwidth-sharing-passive-income/); [Honeygain support](https://support.honeygain.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412730754706-What-is-the-minimum-payout-threshold)). Honeygain, Bright Data and Pawns.app all offer SDKs that developers can embed ([Bright SDK](https://brightdata.com/products/bright-sdk)). The reputational risk in 2026 is sharp. Google disrupted the IPIDEA residential-proxy network in January 2026 and removed apps containing its SDK. Researchers found that more than 550 threat groups used IPIDEA exit nodes in one week ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/29/google_ipidea_crime_network/)). A June 2026 teardown of Bright SDK found vague consent and traffic that bypassed configured VPNs ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/free-apps-are-quietly-turning-smart-tvs.html)). Honeygain's terms allow one account, at most one device per IP, and no VPS, VM or VPN ([visu.network](https://visu.network/blog/honeygain-review/)). Grass paid Season 2 in USDC, citing regulatory uncertainty, and long-time users reported single-digit-dollar payouts ([HokaNews](https://www.hokanews.com/2026/07/grass-airdrop-season-2-enters-final.html)). Storj publishes fiat-pegged storage rates of $1.50/TB-month, but the latest official rate card is from December 2023 ([Storj payouts](https://tardigrade.gitbooks.storj.io/node/payouts)). Mysterium nets slightly negative on a small setup ([gamefi-roi Mysterium](https://gamefi-roi-web.onrender.com/opportunities/mysterium-network-node)).

## A 4090 earns dollars a day, and most other hardware earns cents

The earnings data spans one order of magnitude across hardware and another across workloads. The table combines the dated snapshots in the notes. Mining figures are WhatToMine numbers fetched 15 October 2026 at $0.10/kWh, counting GPU power only ([WhatToMine GPUs](https://whattomine.com/gpus)). CPU figures are XMR revenue computed at about **$0.041 per KH/s per day** from minerstat network data, with XMR at $546.81 ([minerstat XMR](https://minerstat.com/coin/xmr/profitability)) and hashrates from [XMRig benchmarks](https://xmrig.com/benchmark). Power figures for most CPUs are assumptions, not measurements.

|---|---|---|---|
| RTX 4090 | $10.52 (Pearl); ~$1.5–2 without Pearl/Quantus | $9.56 / $7.64 | Vast 5090-class $0.30–0.60/GPU-hr; Salad "$130–150/mo" (undated); ~+$30/mo net at 30% utilization |
| RTX 4070 | $3.29 (Pearl) | $2.93 / $2.21 | — |
| RTX 3060 | $1.39 (Pearl) | $1.15 / $0.67 | Salad's preferred floor card |
| RX 6600 (AMD) | $0.51 (Epic Cash) | $0.24 / **−$0.28** | Salad containers need NVIDIA |
| Ryzen 9 7950X | $1.11 (XMR) | $0.70 / **−$0.11** | — |
| Ryzen 5 5600X | $0.37 (XMR) | $0.19 / **−$0.17** | — |
| Apple M1 | $0.08 (XMR) | ≈$0.00 / −$0.17 | Darkbloom M1 Max 64 GB measured **$0.60/day** |
| Any PC, bandwidth | — | — | $2–25/month |

A product cannot plan around the RTX 4090's numbers. Pearl, an "AI proof-of-useful-work" coin that launched on 27 April 2026, accounts for almost all of today's GPU mining profit. RTX 5090 revenue roughly halved within weeks of launch ([Tom's Hardware](https://tomshardware.com/tech-industry/cryptomining/new-ai-compute-cryptocurrency-pearl-sparks-a-gpu-mining-rush-but-profitability-is-sliding)). A miner developer has warned that an upcoming Pearl upgrade will make consumer GPUs "pretty much useless" for it ([SimeonOnSecurity](https://simeononsecurity.com/articles/pearl-prl-mining-guide-2026/)). Classic GPU coins already lose money: a 3×RTX 3070 rig nets **−$0.94/day on Ravencoin** and −$0.30 on Ergo ([WhatToMine coins](https://whattomine.com/coins)). Kaspa is ASIC territory. Rental figures conflict and depend on utilization, which is the real unknown. Vast's own May 2026 article cites $0.30–0.60/GPU-hr for consumer cards and says listed prices run about 25% above what hosts earn ([Vast earnings article](https://vast.ai/article/how-much-money-can-you-earn-renting-out-your-gpu-on-vast-ai)). A September 2026 net analysis puts a 4090 at 30% utilization and 18.3¢/kWh at about **+$30/month** ([qwe.edu.pl](https://www.qwe.edu.pl/tutorial/gpu-rental-income-real-net/)). SaladCloud charged renters $0.16–0.33/hr for a 4090 in September 2026, so the chef's share is lower than that ([Spheron blog](https://www.spheron.network/blog/saladcloud-pricing-2026-gpu-cost-vs-spheron/)). Salad did raise 4090/5090 rates on 12 September because demand outran supply ([Salad blog](https://blog.salad.com/?p=1534)).

Marketing and measurement diverge everywhere. Darkbloom claims $120–200/month per Mac, but an independent three-day test on an M1 Max measured **$0.60/day**, almost all of it a fixed base reward. Network utilization was about 7%, and the company's own calculator default fell from $75.21/month in June to $12.51 in September ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/24/conjure-cash-with-old-macs-by-linking-them-to-ai-inference-borg/5292002); [miyagadget](https://miyagadget.page/en/blog/2026/06/24/darkbloom-provider-mac-earnings-review-en/)). Salad's Trustpilot reviews repeat the same complaints: earnings below electricity cost, and advertised rates higher than actual ([Trustpilot Salad](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/salad.com)). Electricity decides whether most users profit at all. At $0.30/kWh, common in much of the EU and California, nearly every CPU and every AMD card in the snapshots loses money. Demand-limited networks also dilute: if a wrapper adds thousands of consumer suppliers to a network running at single-digit utilization, per-device pay falls further.

## Wrappers work only where the user keeps the account and the money

A third-party wrapper is technically feasible for most serious options. Vast's CLI and SDK expose host pricing and earnings ([Vast CLI](https://docs.vast.ai/cli/commands)). Clore, Nosana and Golem install headlessly from one-line scripts. io.net accepts a `--token` flag. Darkbloom, XMRig, lolMiner and Excavator all have local CLIs or APIs. Salad is the main exception: its public docs cover only the renter-side API, so a wrapper can launch the app but cannot read earnings ([docs.salad.com](https://docs.salad.com/)). **No platform's terms explicitly permit or forbid third-party host wrappers**, and this is the largest unverified item in the research. The signals that do exist point one way. Darkbloom requires a personally owned device. io.net may disqualify devices that run modified containers ([io.net macOS](https://io.net/docs/guides/workers/install-on-macos.md)). Nosana makes each host accept its terms at registration. Honeygain enforces one account per person. Delegated-operator services, such as NodeOps for Aethir and Livepeer pools, show that some ecosystems tolerate aggregators, but the end user still owns the account ([NodeOps](https://docs.nodeops.network/Guides/Console/aethir)). The safe pattern is for the user to sign into their own backend account or paste their own wallet address. The app orchestrates and never pools.

That same pattern settles most legal exposure. FinCEN's 2019 guidance reads "passing on payments for offering computing power in mining activities" as falling under the integral exemption ([Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2019/05/applying-the-bank-secrecy-act-framework)). Pool operators "who host all the wallets on behalf of pool members", however, appear to need money-services-business registration ([Hunton](https://huntonak.com/blockchain-legal-resource/new-fincen-guidance-on-virtual-currency)). In the EU, MiCA's transitional period ended on 1 July 2026, so only authorized providers may offer crypto custody and transfer ([Elvinger Hoss](https://elvingerhoss.lu/insights/publications/mica-transitional-period-has-come-end-what-comes-next-casps)). DAC8 has required crypto service providers to collect identity and tax numbers since 1 January 2026 ([RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/dac8-and-carf-present-extensive-reporting-challenges-for-crypto-platforms.html)). Incumbents have moved away from custody too. In August 2026 Honeygain's JumpTask moved crypto payouts to a third-party partner and converted balances to fixed-value credits ([Honeygain](https://support.honeygain.com/hc/en-us/articles/28445831396380-What-s-changing-in-JumpTask-2026)). Salad pays in "reward points" that have no cash value ([Salad Terms for Chefs](https://salad.com/terms/chefs/)).

KYC sits with the backends and their payment processors. It is mandatory at NiceHash. Vast delegates it to Wise, PayPal or Stripe, and Darkbloom to Stripe Connect. RunPod required it before it closed to hosts. Kryptex advertises none, and crypto pools ask for nothing beyond an address. In the US, mined coins are ordinary income at fair market value when received, plus self-employment tax if the activity is a business ([CoinTracker](https://cointracker.io/blog/how-is-cryptocurrency-mining-taxed); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/fin-tech/1154806/taxation-of-crypto-mining)). The 1099-NEC/MISC threshold rose to $2,000 for payments after 2025 ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-threshold.html)). The app should therefore export a ledger of timestamp, coin, amount and fair market value at receipt. Mining is banned in China, Nepal and Kuwait and criminalized in Angola. Russia bans it, explicitly including **pool participation**, in Moscow and the Moscow Region from 15 August 2026 to 2032 ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/russia-expands-crypto-mining-ban-moscow-2032); [Unlock-bc](https://www.unlock-bc.com/en/kuwait-bans-bitcoin-mining-citing-power-strain-and-legal-violations)). The app needs a geofence or at least a warning. Store distribution is closed to the mining module. Microsoft Store policy 10.2.6 bars apps that "enable the mining of crypto-currency on device" ([Microsoft Store Policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/uwp/publish/store-policies)). Apple's guidelines 2.4.2 and 3.1.5 ban on-device mining and "unrelated background processes" ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/apple/apple-bans-apps-that-mine-cryptocurrencies)). The Apple sources date from 2018, so check the current wording.

## Build five integrations first, mining before marketplaces

I ranked integrations on four criteria: real money rather than points, coverage of the three target OSes, automation depth (can the app install, start, stop and read earnings), and whether the payout reaches the user without the app touching it. The result is a mining core that works everywhere, plus one best-in-class marketplace per OS.

| Priority | Integration | OSes | Why first | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **XMRig CPU mining → MoneroOcean / unMineable / Kryptex Pool**, paid to user's wallet | Win, Linux, macOS | Only real-money path on all three OSes; GPL engine with HTTP API; non-custodial; low minimums (0.003 XMR ≈ $1.64) | Pennies/day; negative at high tariffs; Qubic and RandomX ASICs keep raising difficulty |
| 2 | **GPU mining engine** (lolMiner/SRBMiner/Rigel downloaded at runtime) with profit switching | Win, Linux | Highest current consumer yield on NVIDIA; fully automatable via local APIs | Pearl dependence; stacked dev fees; AV flags |
| 3 | **Salad handoff** (detect, install, launch, schedule; affiliate link) | Windows | Consumer-native AI containers with PayPal cash-out; near-zero engineering; affiliate revenue | No earnings API; GPU contention with own miner; anti-cheat/VPN conflicts |
| 4 | **Vast.ai host** (install script, `vastai` pricing/earnings, mining as "default job" when unrented) | Linux | Richest host API; USD payouts; serious rigs earn the most | Needs Ubuntu, open ports, near-24/7 uptime; not one-click |
| 5 | **Darkbloom provider** (`darkbloom` CLI wrapper) | macOS (Apple Silicon) | Only paid, USD, Mac-native AI option; beats XMRig on Macs | Public alpha; MDM enrollment; ~$16/month measured; no referral program found |

**XMRig ranks first** because it is the only path that pays real money on every target OS with no third-party account. It ships unmodified as a separate process, which is generally treated as GPL "mere aggregation", so the wrapper can stay proprietary as long as it distributes XMRig's license and source ([OSI license-discuss](https://lists.opensource.org/pipermail/license-discuss_lists.opensource.org/2007-June/012856.html)). MoneroOcean's XMRig fork already benchmarks and switches algorithms against the pool ([Gentoo xmrig-mo](https://gpo.zugaina.org/net-misc/xmrig-mo)). unMineable converts output into 80+ payout coins, so users without a Monero wallet can be paid in a coin they already hold ([unmineable.com](https://www.unmineable.com/)). P2Pool has zero fees but requires a local Monero node, which rules it out for one-click use ([P2Pool](https://github.com/SChernykh/p2pool)). **GPU mining ranks second**: it produces most of the money today, and the app controls every step. It must be marketed as volatile and should switch off automatically when the net is negative. **The Salad handoff ranks third**: it is cheap to build and covers Windows gamers who prefer PayPal to crypto. The app cannot automate Salad, though, and Salad already mines as its own fallback workload ([Salad support](https://support.salad.com/faq/jobs/how-often-do-i-earn-with-salad)). The two must therefore be treated as mutually exclusive owners of the GPU. **Vast ranks fourth**: it serves the Linux-rig power user and lets hosts run their own Docker "default jobs" while idle ([Vast own-machine docs](https://docs.vast.ai/host/using-your-own-machine.md)). That makes rental-versus-mining switching a native feature on Linux. **Darkbloom ranks fifth**: it gives macOS a credible paid option, but its MDM requirement is a heavy trust ask, and the user must go through it in Darkbloom's own flow.

Several options should wait. io.net should wait until onboarding and stake requirements are confirmed live. Nosana and Golem are Linux-only token networks with unmeasured ROI. NiceHash can be an optional payout backend for users who already have KYC'd accounts, but its consumer software is Windows-only and Excavator cannot be redistributed. Bandwidth SDKs should be left out, or at most offered as a separately toggled, explicitly disclosed module, given the 2026 enforcement record. Bittensor, Akash, Flux, Render and points-only AI networks do not fit a consumer one-click app.

## Architecture: a signed shell, downloaded engines, and no custody

### Providers implement one contract, and the scheduler owns the hardware

Each backend should be a provider plugin that declares what it supports and exposes the same lifecycle, so the scheduler can arbitrate between them. A minimal C# shape (my recommendation, not taken from the sources):

```csharp
interface IComputeProvider {
  ProviderCapabilities Capabilities { get; } // OS, CPU/GPU vendor, needs-account, needs-wallet, exclusive-GPU
  Task<InstallResult> InstallAsync(IProgress<string> p, CancellationToken ct); // fetch engine, verify hash
  Task<BenchmarkResult> BenchmarkAsync(HardwareDevice d, CancellationToken ct);
  Task StartAsync(WorkloadPlan plan); Task PauseAsync(); Task StopAsync();
  Task<EarningsSnapshot> GetEarningsAsync(); // pool API, vastai, darkbloom CLI, or "unavailable" (Salad)
  Task UninstallAsync(); // remove engine, exclusions, WSL distros, scheduled tasks
}
```

The scheduler assigns each physical device (each GPU, plus the CPU) to exactly one provider at a time. A Salad or Vast lease is exclusive. Miners run only on devices no lease holds. Profit switching follows the loop the notes document. First, benchmark each algorithm once per device. Then pull revenue per unit of hashrate from WhatToMine, hashrate.no or minerstat. Score each candidate as `revenue − measured incremental watts × tariff − pool/dev fees`. Switch only when the gap clears a hysteresis band, so the app does not thrash between coins ([WhatToMine](https://whattomine.com/coins); [Excavator](https://github.com/nicehash/excavator)). The commercial-use terms of those profitability APIs were not found and must be checked before shipping. Spikes from new coins like Pearl drive both the upside and the risk, so the UI should show the source and age of every estimate. Idle detection, fullscreen/game pause, and autostart through Task Scheduler, launchd and systemd user units are standard patterns, but the notes did not verify them.

### Electricity accounting must measure GPUs and estimate CPUs

Measuring power is easy on NVIDIA. `nvmlDeviceGetPowerUsage` returns whole-board milliwatts without admin rights and is callable from .NET via P/Invoke, and the energy counter on Volta and newer GPUs is more accurate than polling ([NVML API](https://docs.nvidia.com/deploy/nvml-api/latest/api/group__nvmlDeviceQueries.html); [ML.ENERGY](https://ml.energy/blog/energy/measurement/measuring-gpu-energy-best-practices/)). CPUs are harder. On Linux, RAPL counters have been root-only since kernel 5.10 because of the PLATYPUS side channel, and opening them needs a udev-rule group grant ([Intel INTEL-SA-00389](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/software-security-guidance/advisory-guidance/running-average-power-limit-energy-reporting.html); [CodeCarbon](https://docs.codecarbon.io/latest/how-to/enable-rapl/)). On macOS, `powermetrics` requires root and returns estimates ([asitop](https://github.com/tlkh/asitop)). The pragmatic design is measured GPU watts, plus a modeled CPU delta above the PC's idle baseline, multiplied by a user-entered $/kWh (with country defaults). A "never run at a net loss" toggle should be on by default. Accounting is the product's honesty mechanism. The CPU table above shows that the same Ryzen flips from +$0.70 to −$0.11 a day as the tariff moves from $0.10 to $0.30.

### Separate the clean shell from the miners that antivirus will flag

Microsoft Defender classifies miners as `PUA:Win32/CoinMiner`, and no amount of signing will clear them ([Microsoft Answers](https://answers.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/forum/all/windows-defender-puawin32coinminer-active-false/e2f3213a-a8f6-4a0d-b357-9097bff65548)). Since 2024, EV certificates no longer bypass SmartScreen, so both EV and OV certificates build reputation over time. Azure Artifact Signing is the cheaper signing route ([Microsoft Learn: SmartScreen](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/smartscreen-reputation); [code signing options](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/code-signing-options)). Ship a small signed Avalonia shell that never contains miner code. After explicit opt-in, download engines into a dedicated folder from official release URLs, verify their hashes, and add a narrow, consent-based Defender exclusion for that folder only. Never copy Kryptex's advice to turn off PUA blocking globally ([Kryptex](https://www.kryptex.com/de/articles/not-a-virus-en)). Downloading at runtime also respects closed-miner licenses, which forbid redistribution or modification. NiceHash QuickMiner limits itself to first-party Excavator plus XMRig to reduce detections, which supports keeping the engine set small ([NiceHash #204](https://github.com/nicehash/NiceHashQuickMiner/issues/204)). XMRig's Windows package bundles the WinRing0 kernel driver. The notes infer that it may hit Microsoft's vulnerable-driver blocklist, so ship without it and accept the lower hashrate ([XMRig GitHub](https://github.com/xmrig/xmrig)). On macOS, distribute a notarized Developer ID build outside the App Store. Notarization rules for apps that download helper binaries were not researched.

### Avalonia fits, but Linux tray behavior needs testing

Avalonia's TrayIcon with NativeMenu works on Windows and macOS and is confirmed on "some Linux distributions (confirmed Ubuntu)". A main-window fallback is therefore mandatory on GNOME-style desktops ([Avalonia TrayIcon](https://docs.avaloniaui.net/controls/navigation/trayicon)). One C# codebase can hold the UI, NVML P/Invoke, process control and the provider host. Electron or Tauri would need a native sidecar for those anyway. The notes flag, without verification, that .NET 9 support has ended and that the current LTS release is the right target.

### Payouts stay non-custodial

Every provider pays the user directly: the user's wallet address for pools, or the user's own Salad, Vast, Darkbloom or NiceHash account. The app takes its revenue from three sources that never require holding user funds. First, a disclosed developer fee, implemented as a published XMRig fork whose donation slice points at the app's wallet, which the GPL permits if source is offered. Second, backend affiliate programs. Third, an optional subscription. Comparable rig-management tools charge $0.50 per GPU per month up to $3/month (Hive OS) or about $2 per worker per month (minerstat) ([Hiveon billing](https://hiveon.com/knowledge-base/faq/billing_in_hive_os/billing); [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/awesome-miner/pricing)). If a unified fiat balance ever becomes essential, follow Salad and JumpTask: non-cash credits plus a licensed payout partner that handles KYC, signed off by counsel.

## Fund referrals with foregone fees, not cash

*This section is a recommendation derived from the findings above. The notes contain no research on referral-program law, multi-level-marketing rules, FTC disclosure, or app-store referral policies. Statements about those topics are design judgment and need legal review. Statements about revenue, custody and fraud precedents are sourced where cited.*

### The revenue pool is cents to a few dollars per user per month

Everything the app can pay out has to come from its own thin revenue. Three numbers set the budget. Backend affiliate income is uncertain: Salad runs an application-based affiliate program with unpublished tiers ([Salad Affiliate](https://salad.com/affiliate)). unMineable's referrer share is reported at about 0.25% of referred miners' rewards, from conflicting promotional sources ([gist](https://gist.github.com/svv3085/b4ee22f7f19d7807b261288fe8847541)). No referral terms were found for Vast, Clore or Darkbloom. A disclosed dev fee of 1–2% on the earnings in the table above yields, by my arithmetic, about **$0.10/month for a Ryzen 5**, **$0.30 for a 16-core Ryzen**, **$0.40 for an RTX 3060**, **$0.60 for a non-Pearl 4090**, and **$3–6 for a 4090 while Pearl lasts**. Salad, Vast and Darkbloom users generate no dev fee at all, because those backends pay users directly. Subscriptions, priced like Hive OS or minerstat, top out at a few dollars per rig per month and will convert only power users. A typical user is therefore worth a few dollars over their lifetime. A fixed cash bounty of $5 per signup would lose money on most referrals and would directly reward bot farms.

### Recommended structure: single level, two-sided, proportional, non-custodial

The structure that fits these economics pays both sides with value the app already controls, and pays it only after verified contribution:

| Reward mechanism | Funded by | Custody / money-transmission exposure | Fraud exposure | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Two-sided dev-fee holiday** (e.g., both pay 0% for 30–60 days once the referee logs N verified compute-hours) | Foregone fee revenue | None: no money moves | Low: self-referral only saves one's own fee | **Ship first** |
| **Referrer receives a share of the app's fee on the referee for 6–12 months**, delivered as a hashing slice pointed at the referrer's own wallet | The app's fee slice (the referee's total fee does not rise) | Low: the app never receives or holds the referrer's reward, but the redirect must be disclosed and reviewed by counsel | Low: self-referral returns less than the fee paid | **Ship for mining users** |
| **Premium-tier months** as a reward | Subscription margin | None | Low | Good for non-mining (Salad/Vast/Darkbloom) users |
| Cash or gift-card bounty via a licensed payout partner | Marketing budget | Partner handles KYC; app still has income-reporting duties above thresholds | High: bounties attract farms | Later, small, capped |
| Crypto bounty from an app-held treasury wallet | Treasury | Higher: the app moves crypto to users, which runs into MiCA's custody/transfer rules | High | Avoid |
| Pooling users' mining output and redistributing it as rewards | Users' earnings | High: matches the "host all the wallets" pattern FinCEN flags | High | **Never** |
| Multi-level overrides (rewards on referrals of referrals) | Recruitment | — | High; pyramid optics | **Never** |

The proportional fee share works like unMineable's model, which reportedly splits 0.5% to the platform, 0.25% to the referrer and 0.25% back to the miner ([gist](https://gist.github.com/svv3085/b4ee22f7f19d7807b261288fe8847541)). The app can reproduce that split with no custody at all. The referee's miner simply spends a disclosed sliver of time on the referrer's wallet instead of the app's, exactly as XMRig's donation mechanism already does for its developers ([XMRig GitHub](https://github.com/xmrig/xmrig)). Its key property is that fraud does not pay. Because the referrer's reward is a fraction of the fee the referee actually generated, self-referral or a bot farm can only recover part of its own fee, never profit. The economics are self-policing. Rewards received this way are mining income to the referrer at receipt ([CoinTracker](https://cointracker.io/blog/how-is-cryptocurrency-mining-taxed)), and the app's tax ledger should label them as such.

Backend referral codes complicate the design. Salad accepts a referral code only at signup and gives the referee a 2× earning bonus until $4 earned ([Salad referral](https://support.salad.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042398312)). unMineable cuts its fee to 0.75% when a referral code is used. Each backend signup therefore has room for one code. The app should use its own affiliate code there to fund the program, and pass the backend's sign-up bonus to the friend as part of the welcome offer. It should not let users inject personal backend codes through the app, since the backends' wrapper terms are unknown.

### What to avoid

Avoid any structure that pays for recruitment rather than compute. That means no rewards for downstream levels, no ranks earned through recruiting, and no requirement to buy anything (tokens, node licences, stake) to join or earn. The notes already flag node-licence sales and required staking as raising securities questions ([Decrypt on Aethir node sale](https://decrypt.co/218993/aethir-unveils-its-first-decentralized-ai-node-sale)), so referral rewards must never be tied to them. Avoid earnings claims in share links that the product cannot back up. Measured results run well below marketing (Darkbloom's $120–200 claimed versus about $16 measured), and "advertised rate > actual" is the most common complaint about Salad. Share cards should show the referrer's own measured net, or a dated range. Avoid running referral campaigns in jurisdictions where the app geofences mining, such as Russia's banned regions, where pool participation itself is prohibited. On app stores, the mining module cannot ship through the Microsoft Store or Mac App Store anyway. Any store-distributed "lite" edition should keep referral rewards away from ratings or reviews and should be checked against current store rules, which the notes did not cover. Referral links should be disclosed as referral links. The notes contain no FTC-specific research, so that is a best-practice recommendation.

### Anti-abuse rests on verified contribution and device uniqueness

Rewards should vest only on contribution that a third party has verified, never on installs or signups: pool-accepted shares to the referee's address, or backend-reported earnings, sustained over a 14–30-day holding period, with clawback if activity stops. The app should count unique physical devices (NVML GPU UUID plus a hashed machine identifier) rather than accounts. It should flag self-referral when referrer and referee share a payout address, a device fingerprint or a household IP. Since every payout goes to a user-supplied address, a shared address is a near-certain match. It should exclude virtual machines and data-center IP ranges from reward eligibility, following the backends' own precedents: Honeygain bans VPS, VM and VPN use and allows one device per IP, and Darkbloom requires a personally owned device and bans cloud arbitrage ([visu.network](https://visu.network/blog/honeygain-review/); [miyagadget](https://miyagadget.page/en/blog/2026/06/24/darkbloom-provider-mac-earnings-review-en/)). Annual reward caps per referrer and rate limits on new referrals per day bound the worst case. Fake "boosted earnings" plug-ins already circulate for earn networks ([PANews](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/afahxv56gfqm)). Referral links should therefore deep-link only to the app's signed official download, and the app should publish hashes for every engine it fetches.

## Conclusion

Selling idle compute in 2026 is mainly a trust and accounting problem, not a search for the best backend. The best-paying consumer workload is a speculative coin that may lock consumer GPUs out within months, and the AI networks that pay cash run at single-digit utilization. A new app therefore cannot win on the size of payouts. It can win on two things incumbents do badly: running the same way on Windows, Linux and macOS, and telling users honestly, in measured watts and dollars, whether running is worth it at their tariff. The integration order follows from that. A non-custodial XMRig core gives every user something real on day one. Each later integration adds a best-in-class paid option per OS without making the app a custodian.

The referral program should follow the same principle. Because each user brings in so little revenue, cash bounties are both unaffordable and an invitation to fraud. A two-sided fee holiday plus a proportional, hash-sliced share of the app's own fee costs nothing up front, scales with real compute delivered, and makes self-referral pointless. The open questions to settle before launch are each backend's wrapper and affiliate terms, the commercial terms of the profitability-data APIs, the current Apple and Microsoft store wording, and counsel's view on hash-slice referral rewards. Of these, the backend wrapper terms are the riskiest unknown and the cheapest to resolve: one email to each platform's partnerships team.
