# Route every hash through Kryptex, keep an exit

Otium can make its money flow nearly automatic by moving **all balance-account mining, CPU and GPU, onto Kryptex Pool under the operator's Kryptex account**, where every coin Otium mines except Quai is auto-converted to Bitcoin. That removes MoneroOcean, the operator's Monero wallet, the subaddress allocator and the XMR-to-BTC swap problem in one move. The question is not whether Kryptex can carry everything; it can. The questions are three gaps in what Kryptex documents. First, Kryptex publishes no automatic withdrawal or withdrawal API: BTC sits in the Kryptex account until someone confirms a withdrawal by email, so one weekly manual sweep remains. Second, Kryptex's public per-worker API refused a username instead of a wallet address in a live test, so Otium's existing Kryptex crediting is unproven for account mining and must be tested before anything else moves. Third, its terms are silent on operators aggregating thousands of users and let Kryptex end service "for any reason". The only other working pool that turns RandomX into Bitcoin is **unMineable**, which pays BTC straight to an external address once a day. That makes it the right pre-wired failover, but not the primary, because of its double fee layer, unverified worker records and unreadable terms. Every algo-switching alternative has collapsed: Zergpool and ProHashing shut down, zpool dropped RandomX, and MiningDutch's RandomX is empty. On bandwidth, **Mysterium is the only crypto-paying option worth building**, with Kryptex itself as the precedent. MYST is so illiquid that conversion to BTC must be batched, and it caps how large that revenue line can grow. The end state is one pool, one Bitcoin treasury, a weekly sweep and a weekly MYST batch, plus a failover that keeps paying Bitcoin if Kryptex goes away.

## Only two pools still turn CPU RandomX into Bitcoin

The market for "mine RandomX, get paid in BTC" has shrunk to two names. **Zergpool** returned HTTP 522 on 16 October 2026. Secondary sources say its pool services ended in September 2025, and October 2025 posts report miners who never received their shutdown payouts ([MEXC glossary](https://www.mexc.com/crypto-glossary/article/zergpool-135531); [r/zergpool mirror](https://red.applefritter.com/r/zergpool)). **ProHashing's** homepage now says it has shut down, and its API reports that it "has been permanently retired" ([prohashing.com](https://prohashing.com/)). **zpool** still pays BTC for CPU algorithms such as GhostRider (7,343 workers) and VerusHash, but its live status API lists **no RandomX** ([zpool status API](https://zpool.ca/api/status)). **MiningDutch** lists RandomX at a 2% fee with zero hashrate and zero workers ([MiningDutch API](https://www.mining-dutch.nl/api/status/)). 2Miners has no XMR pool ([2Miners API, 404](https://xmr.2miners.com/api/stats)). HeroMiners runs a plain XMR pool with no exchange ([HeroMiners API](https://monero.herominers.com/api/stats)). NiceHash pays only BTC, but it has required KYC for all users since late 2024, according to a competitor's article and user reports ([Kryptex](https://www.kryptex.com/articles/nicehash-alternative-en); [Trustpilot](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/www.nicehash.com?page=6)). Building on the survivors of this category is a counterparty bet. Zergpool's disorderly exit is the warning.

The two that remain differ mainly in how the Bitcoin leaves.

| | **Kryptex (account login)** | **unMineable** |
|---|---|---|
| RandomX scheme and fee | PPS+ 1% (no luck variance) | 1% (0.75% with a referral code, unverified third-party claim); conversion spread undisclosed |
| Size | ~1.02 GH/s, ~15.5% of Monero's network, 30,332 workers | not published |
| How BTC arrives | Credited to the operator's kryptex.com balance; withdrawal is user-initiated and confirmed by email | Paid automatically once a day to an external BTC address, "no network fees" |
| Minimum | Withdraw from 0.00025 BTC, fee 0.00003 BTC | 0.00075 BTC (~$61) |
| Per-worker stats | `/{coin}/api/v3/miner/workers/{address}` and a per-worker chart in **10-minute buckets** (144 points per day) | `/v4/account/{uuid}/workers`: per-algorithm worker list plus reported and calculated hashrate charts; per-worker fields unverified |
| Open problem | Public API returned "You do not have permission" for a username instead of an address | Terms and FAQ return 404; worker limits and aggregation policy unknown |

Sources: [Kryptex XMR pool](https://pool.kryptex.com/en/xmr); [Kryptex pool info API](https://pool.kryptex.com/xmr/api/v1/pool/info); [Kryptex workers API (live)](https://pool.kryptex.com/xmr/api/v3/miner/workers/48edfHu7V9Z84YzzMa6fUueoELZ9ZRXq9VetWzYGzKt52XU5xvqgzYnDK9URnRoJMk1j8nLwEVsaSWJ4fhdUyZijBGUicoD); [Kryptex fees](https://www.kryptex.com/en/fees); [unMineable BTC](https://unmineable.com/coins/BTC); [unMineable API (live)](https://api.unminable.com/v4/address/bc1qxy2kgdygjrsqtzq2n0yrf2493p83kkfjhx0wlh?coin=BTC); [Docker Hub unMineable image](https://hub.docker.com/r/thechristech/unmineable).

Kryptex wins on almost everything Otium's crediting cares about. Its **PPS+ scheme pays for shares rather than blocks**, so the operator stops carrying the pool luck that MoneroOcean's PPLNS imposes today. Its per-worker chart already arrives in the same 10-minute rhythm as Otium's crediting loop, with hashrate in H/s and share counts per bucket ([Kryptex OpenAPI](https://pool.kryptex.com/openapi.yaml)). It is about **four times MoneroOcean's size** (1.02 GH/s against about 244 MH/s) ([MoneroOcean API](https://api.moneroocean.stream/pool/stats)). Otium's 14-character worker ids ("ot" plus 12 lower-case letters and digits) fit Kryptex's rule of at most 32 Latin letters and digits ([Kryptex account mining](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/account-mining-en)). Kryptex also reports no equivalent of MoneroOcean's limit of about 50 separately tracked workers per address, which is the reason Otium's `PoolWalletAllocator` exists. That absence still needs confirming in writing.

unMineable wins on one thing: the money moves by itself. With `BTC:<treasury address>.<workerid>` as the login, RandomX output lands in the bitcoind treasury daily with no human step ([unMineable BTC](https://unmineable.com/coins/BTC)). Its API exposes a per-algorithm `randomx` workers list without authentication. Field names and history granularity could not be checked because the test address had no active workers ([unMineable API](https://api.unminable.com/v4/address/bc1qxy2kgdygjrsqtzq2n0yrf2493p83kkfjhx0wlh?coin=BTC)). Kryptex, a competitor, calls it a proxy pool where "miners pay double fee" ([Kryptex](https://www.kryptex.com/articles/unmineable-alternative-en)). Its 0.00075 BTC threshold also means about $61 must accrue before each payout. That is a fine profile for a failover and a weak one for the primary.

Switching CPU coins does not pay. hashrate.no puts a Ryzen 9 7950X at **$0.84 a day on Monero**, $0.89 on Tari's RandomX chain (+6%) and $0.98 on Epic Cash (+17%), while Zephyr, Salvium, QRL and VerusHash all pay less ([hashrate.no 7950X](https://www.hashrate.no/cpus/7950X)). Epic has no BTC-converting pool. Tari is mineable with unmodified XMRig on Kryptex and auto-converts to BTC, but at PROP (luck-exposed) with a 200 XTM minimum ([Kryptex XTM-RX](https://pool.kryptex.com/xtm-rx)). A 6% edge that comes and goes is worth a server-side toggle later, not a design decision now. Qubic claimed roughly three times Monero's yield in mid-2025, but those are self-reported figures from a project tied to the August 2025 "51% attack" episode, mined through a proprietary client ([Qubic blog](https://qubic.org/blog-detail/why-qubic-is-now-the-top-profitable-coin-to-mine-in-2025); [arXiv 2512.01437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01437v2)). It does not fit Otium.

## Kryptex already lists every profitable GPU coin, and MoneroOcean's add almost nothing

GPU revenue in October 2026 is concentrated in two new coins. On an RTX 3080 at $0.10/kWh, hashrate.no shows **Pearl at $3.80 a day revenue ($3.42 profit) and Quantus at $2.96 ($2.62)**. Next come NOID ($1.81) and EPIC ($1.73), with Conflux at $0.78 and Xelis at $0.37 ([hashrate.no RTX 3080](https://www.hashrate.no/gpus/3080)). MoneroOcean's GPU set is near or below break-even: Ergo earns $0.25 revenue for −$0.09 profit, and Quai-KawPow $0.50 for −$0.10. That confirms why Otium already marks MoneroOcean's GPU algorithms "free power only". On AMD the order flips. An RX 6800 XT makes **$0.91 a day profit on Quantus but only $0.39 on Pearl**, with EPIC and Qubitcoin at $0.53 each ([hashrate.no RX 6800 XT](https://www.hashrate.no/gpus/6800xt)). WhatToMine's live index agrees on the top two: Pearl 6165, Quantus 5186, then Tari-C29 at 2333 and everything else below ([WhatToMine coins.json](https://whattomine.com/coins.json)).

Kryptex carries Pearl, Quantus, Tari-C29, Iron Fish, Xelis and Conflux, which is the core of that list. It also carries weaker coins (Ergo, Ravencoin, Nexa, Neurai, Quai-KawPow) and three CPU RandomX chains (Monero, Tari-RX, Zephyr). Every coin except the three Quai variants shows "Auto-convert: Yes" ([Kryptex Pool](https://pool.kryptex.com/); [Kryptex API index](https://pool.kryptex.com/api/v1/index)). The coins Kryptex lacks (EPIC, NOID, Qubitcoin, Zano, Firo, Evrmore) mostly matter on AMD cards, and the gap is under $0.50 a day per card. Every alternative for them pays in the mined coin, so Otium would need its own altcoin-to-BTC swap. THORChain does not list those coins, so that means an exchange account and likely KYC.

The alternatives to Kryptex each fail one test. **unMineable** pays BTC, ETH, BNB, SOL or USDT straight to a wallet and lists Pearl, Xelis, Iron Fish and Conflux among its BTC-earning algorithms. But it **lacks Quantus**, and its workers API has no `pearl` or `xelis` key, which leaves per-worker crediting for the top coin unproven ([unMineable BTC](https://unmineable.com/coins/BTC); [unMineable address API](https://api.unminable.com/v4/address/1A1zP1eP5QGefi2DMPTfTL5SLmv7DivfNa?coin=BTC)). **WoolyPooly** carries Pearl, Xelis, Conflux, Zano and Firo, and HeroMiners and LuckyPool carry Pearl, but all pay in the mined coin ([WoolyPooly API](https://api.woolypooly.com/api/stats); [Kryptex Pearl pools](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/pearl-mining-pools-en)). **zpool** has no Pearl, Quantus, Xelis, RandomX, FishHash or Autolykos ([zpool API](https://www.zpool.ca/api/status)). SRBMiner covers every Kryptex GPU algorithm Otium would use (pearlhash, quantus, xelishash, fishhash, cuckaroo29), so one GPU binary still suffices. Its own devfee, 2% on Quantus since 3.6.7, stacks on top of the pool fee ([SRBMiner releases](https://github.com/doktor83/SRBMiner-Multi/releases)).

So yes: **all of Otium's balance-account mining can run through one Kryptex account and arrive as BTC**. CPU XMRig connects to `xmr-eu.kryptex.network:7029` (TLS on 8029) with standard stratum ([Kryptex XMR pool info](https://pool.kryptex.com/xmr/api/v1/pool/info)). The account login `krxXXXXXX.worker` credits "all earnings ... to your kryptex.com account in Bitcoins" ([Kryptex account mining](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/account-mining-en)). Mining to a plain wallet address gives a "traditional mining pool without auto-conversion", so the account login is essential ([Kryptex vs Kryptex Pool](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/kryptex-vs-kryptex-pool-en/_payload.json)). Three caveats decide whether this is wise.

**The Bitcoin exit is manual.** Kryptex documents withdrawals in BTC on-chain (0.00003 BTC fee, 0.00025 minimum), Lightning (2%, capped at 0.005 BTC), ETH, LTC, BNB, TRX, USDT and USDC. It documents no automatic withdrawal, and "to withdraw funds from Kryptex, email confirmation is required" ([Kryptex fees](https://www.kryptex.com/en/fees); [Kryptex account mining](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/account-mining-en)). Kryptex's comparison page puts it more bluntly: account payouts are "user-initiated and manually confirmed", while the pool's automatic payouts to a fixed address apply only to wallet mining, which does not convert ([Kryptex vs Kryptex Pool](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/kryptex-vs-kryptex-pool-en)). Otium's README currently tells the operator to "point Kryptex's BTC payouts at the bitcoind wallet". On Kryptex's own documentation that is not possible for account mining, and the setup notes should say "sweep weekly" until Kryptex says otherwise. At about $81,500 per BTC, each on-chain sweep costs about **$2.45** ([mempool.space prices](https://mempool.space/api/v1/prices)). Sweeping when the balance passes roughly 0.0025 BTC keeps that under about 1.2% while capping custodial exposure at about $200.

**Per-worker crediting for account mining is unproven.** Otium's `KryptexClient` calls `/{coin}/api/v3/miner/workers/{account}` and the per-worker chart with the account name. In a live test, a username in that position returned `{"detail":"You do not have permission to perform this action."}`, while wallet addresses worked ([Kryptex workers API](https://pool.kryptex.com/xmr/api/v3/miner/workers/48edfHu7V9Z84YzzMa6fUueoELZ9ZRXq9VetWzYGzKt52XU5xvqgzYnDK9URnRoJMk1j8nLwEVsaSWJ4fhdUyZijBGUicoD)). The test username was not Otium's, so the endpoint might answer for the account's owner or need an authenticated route. Until a real `krx…` account returns data, Otium's existing Pearl and Quantus crediting is also unverified. The same test should check one parsing detail: the workers endpoint returns its average hashrates as strings, while `KryptexClient` accepts only JSON numbers for the chart's `hashrate`. If the chart also uses strings, every point would be dropped and credits would be zero.

**The terms permit it by silence, not by consent.** Kryptex's terms contain no clause on reselling hashrate, operators, aggregators or bundling into third-party software. They do let Kryptex "modify, improve or discontinue any of its services at its sole discretion and without notice", and either party can terminate "at any time for any reason" ([Kryptex terms](https://www.kryptex.com/terms-and-conditions)). Kryptex also runs the consumer app Otium competes with. It auto-switches algorithms, pays users in BTC with no KYC, and mines CPU RandomX ([Kryptex Miner](https://www.kryptex.com/pro-app); [Kryptex SAL→BTC](https://www.kryptex.com/en/news/sal-btc-en)). That makes written permission worth more than usual.

## Mysterium is the only bandwidth income that pays the operator in crypto worth building

Most bandwidth programs that "pay in crypto" fail Otium's operator model. **Traffmonetizer** pays USDT on TRON with a $10 minimum, which is the cleanest crypto rail, because THORChain has swapped native TRON USDT to BTC since about October 2025 ([Jivaro](https://jivaro.net/resources/share-internet-bandwidth-for-money/); [THORChain blog](https://blog.thorchain.org/tron-integration-complete-native-trx-usdt-swaps-live-on-thorchain)). But it is a consumer app with no publisher SDK and no per-device API, and no permission for operator use could be found. **Packetshare** pays USDT on an unstated network with a $20 minimum ([Honeygain ES comparison](https://www.honeygain.com/es/blog/app-para-compartir-ancho-de-banda/)). **Bitping** pays SOL, at an estimated $0–5 per device per month ([Bitping blog](https://bitping.com/blog/bitpings-solana-transition); [CashPilot guide](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GeiserX/CashPilot/HEAD/docs/guides/bitping.md)). **Sentinel** pays its P2P token, which hit an all-time low on 4 August 2026, and payments are "substantially subsidized" ([Sentinel docs](https://docs.sentinel.co/dvpn-nodes/earnings); [CoinMarketCap](https://coinmarketcap.com/currencies/sentinel/)). Honeygain's crypto option is the JMPT token through JumpTask. Pawns.app discontinued bandwidth sharing, and EarnApp, PacketStream and Proxyrack pay fiat only ([Jivaro](https://jivaro.net/resources/share-internet-bandwidth-for-money/)). Grass and most DePIN "bandwidth" networks still pay points or airdrops, which count as zero.

**Mysterium** is the exception on every structural test. It pays natively in MYST. It has an official app-integration client (`mysteriumnetwork/mystnodes-sdk-client`, run with a partner token "obtained from mystnodes team", advertising Windows, macOS, Linux and Android) ([Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/mysteriumnetwork/mystnodes-sdk-client)). And it has a live precedent for Otium's exact model. Since December 2023 Kryptex has run full Mysterium nodes inside its Windows app, and "Kryptex converts your MYST into Bitcoin once you accumulate 5 MYST" before users withdraw BTC as usual ([Kryptex news](https://www.kryptex.com/en/news/kryptex_x_mysterium); [Kryptex getting started](https://www.kryptex.com/en/articles/mysterium-getting-started-en)). Each user's PC runs its own node identity, which gives the cleanest per-device attribution of any bandwidth option. The node's Web UI can be limited to "B2B Data Scraping, B2B VPN and Data Transfer" traffic from Mysterium's business partners, which Mysterium recommends as "clean and safe", instead of the anonymous "Mysterium Dark" public exit ([Kryptex getting started](https://www.kryptex.com/en/articles/mysterium-getting-started-en)).

The weak points are size and liquidity. MystNodes claims **$8–25 a month for an always-on home node**. Independent figures are closer to **$1.20–2.33 a month**, and the whole network has paid out only $568,019 "to date" ([MystNodes blog](https://www.mystnodes.com/blog/how-much-can-you-earn-sharing-bandwidth); [DePIN Scan](https://depinscan.io/projects/mysterium); [PaidFromSurveys](https://paidfromsurveys.com/mysterium-node-review)). Settlement is in MYST on Polygon after a 20% network fee, and balances auto-settle at 5 MYST ([Jivaro](https://jivaro.net/resources/share-internet-bandwidth-for-money/); [MystNodes help](https://help.mystnodes.com/en/articles/8005144-where-do-i-check-and-withdraw-my-earnings)). On 16 October 2026 MYST traded at **$0.0797, down about half since March**. Its market cap was about $2.6M and 24-hour volume about $25.8k. The two usable on-chain pools, Quickswap MYST/USDC.e on Polygon and Uniswap V3 MYST/WETH on Ethereum, each hold only about **$2.5k of depth within ±2%** ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/mysterium); [MEXC](https://www.mexc.com/price-prediction/MYST)). THORChain does not support Polygon ([THORChain blog](https://blog.thorchain.org/tron-integration-complete-native-trx-usdt-swaps-live-on-thorchain)). The no-KYC route to the treasury is therefore two hops. MYST on Polygon goes to USDC.e on Quickswap, is bridged to Ethereum or Base, and is swapped by THORChain to BTC. Alternatively, MYST on Ethereum goes to WETH on Uniswap and then through THORChain to BTC.

That liquidity sets a ceiling. A thousand nodes at the independent $1.20 a month produce about $40 a day of MYST, which daily small tranches can sell without much price impact. Ten thousand nodes at the vendor's $8 would produce about $2,700 a day. That is roughly half of Quickswap's daily volume, and Otium would become the market. Mysterium is worth building for a fleet of hundreds to low thousands. Beyond that, the conversion limit binds before demand does. Because MYST lost half its value in seven months, Otium should **credit users from MYST actually converted to BTC**, not at spot when earned.

Honeygain stays as the fiat-paid partner already integrated. The earlier research did not establish its publisher payout method. If it pays fiat, that revenue reaches the crypto treasury only through a manual BTC purchase. It is a small, monthly step, but it is the one bandwidth flow that cannot be made crypto-native. Traffmonetizer becomes worth revisiting only if it grants written operator permission. Its USDT-TRC20 payouts would then flow into THORChain's TRON.USDT pool (about $208k deep in an undated snapshot) with no DEX hop ([deving.zone](https://deving.zone/thorchain/blockchains/tron)).

## The simplified setup: one pool, one treasury, two weekly chores

The recommended end state routes every operator-owned revenue stream to Bitcoin before it touches Otium's books. Own-wallet users keep mining on MoneroOcean to their own address, which never touches the operator's money.

| Stream | Pool or partner | Miner | Arrives as | Path into the bitcoind treasury | Human step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CPU, balance accounts | **Kryptex XMR**, account login, PPS+ 1% | XMRig `rx/0` | BTC in Kryptex account | Sweep withdrawal | Confirm the weekly withdrawal email |
| GPU, balance accounts | **Kryptex** Pearl and Quantus, plus Xelis, Iron Fish, Conflux and Tari-C29 when the policy scores them up; never Quai | SRBMiner | BTC in Kryptex account | Same sweep | Same |
| Failover (CPU and Pearl) | **unMineable**, `BTC:<treasury>.<workerid>` | Same binaries | BTC on-chain, daily, ≥0.00075 BTC | Direct | None |
| Own-wallet users | MoneroOcean to the user's address | XMRig | XMR to the user | Not Otium's money | None |
| Bandwidth, crypto | **Mysterium** nodes, B2B traffic only, partner token | myst node | MYST on Polygon, settled at 5 MYST | DEX → USDC → bridge → THORChain → BTC | Weekly batch until scripted |
| Bandwidth, fiat | Honeygain SDK (existing) | — | Partner payout | Buy BTC | Monthly, if paid in fiat |

The code changes are moderate, because Otium's GPU path already works this way. The table lists them by component.

| Component | Change |
|---|---|
| **App: CPU pool route** | `PoolEndpoint.MoneroOcean` is hard-coded in `Otium.Providers.XMRig`. Balance accounts should take their CPU route (host, port, TLS, login template `"{account}.{workerId}"`) from the server, by extending the GPU policy into a mining policy with a CPU section. That is what makes failover a server switch rather than an app release. Own-wallet mode keeps MoneroOcean. |
| **Server: Kryptex CPU coin** | Add `randomx → { Coin = "xmr", Host = "xmr-eu.kryptex.network", Port = 7029 or 8029 }` to `KryptexOptions.Coins`, priced from WhatToMine's Monero entry like the GPU coins. `KryptexCreditor` is already coin-generic, and its median-spacing integration handles 10-minute buckets. Later, `xtm-rx` can sit behind the same XMRig `rx/0` as a profitability toggle. |
| **Server: Kryptex client** | Use whatever authenticated or account-scoped endpoint Kryptex confirms. Accept string-encoded hashrates. Read the pool's scheme per coin so PPS+ and PROP coins can carry different haircuts. |
| **Server: retire balance-mode Monero** | Stop `PoolWalletAllocator` assigning subaddresses to new balance accounts. Leave `EarningsCreditor` running until MoneroOcean's last payout clears, then disable it and drop the `monero-wallet-rpc` dependency for balance mode. Remove the MoneroOcean GPU route for balance accounts; it is free-power-only and pays XMR. |
| **Server: sweep ledger** | Kryptex exposes no balance API for accounts. Track an "unswept Kryptex" estimate: gross credited at Kryptex minus BTC received in bitcoind from Kryptex (`listtransactions` on a dedicated receive label). Alert when it passes the sweep threshold or seven days. |
| **Server: unMineable adapter** | `IUnmineableApi` (`/v4/address/{addr}?coin=BTC` → uuid; `/v4/account/{uuid}/workers`) plus a creditor mirroring `KryptexCreditor`, and a policy route that the server flips to when Kryptex's stratum or API fails for more than N minutes. |
| **App and server: Mysterium** | A `MysteriumBandwidthProvider` (own consent screen, B2B-only traffic types, updater disabled) and a per-identity creditor. Both must be built against the partner terms, since per-identity earnings via the local node API and a shared beneficiary are unverified. Credit on conversion, not on accrual. |
| **Docs** | Replace "point Kryptex's BTC payouts at the bitcoind wallet" with the weekly sweep, and record the Kryptex/Quantus ("qtc") versus Qubitcoin ("QTC") ticker clash. |

What stays manual is short. Each week someone confirms one Kryptex withdrawal email, and converts the week's MYST batch until that path is scripted. Given the thin pools, a human watching slippage is reasonable for a while. If Honeygain pays fiat, someone buys BTC monthly. The final MoneroOcean XMR balance needs one swap to BTC during migration. Held cash-outs still go to review as before.

### Migration in five phases, each with an exit test

**Phase 0, week 1: prove the account path.** Create the production Kryptex account. Mine XMR with XMRig and Pearl with SRBMiner on two or three test workers using the `krx….worker` login. Confirm that the workers list and per-worker chart return data for that account, and in which units and types. Do one BTC withdrawal to bitcoind and time it. In parallel, mine RandomX to an unMineable BTC address for a day to capture the real worker JSON. Send the Kryptex, unMineable and MystNodes questions below. *Exit test:* per-worker 10-minute points for an account worker that integrate to within a few percent of XMRig's own reported hashes.

**Phase 1, weeks 2–3: ship the server-side CPU route.** Release the app version that reads the CPU route from the policy, while the policy still points at MoneroOcean. Then flip a canary of new balance accounts to Kryptex XMR. *Exit test:* a week where Kryptex-credited CPU earnings reconcile against BTC received from the sweep, within the auto-exchange spread.

**Phase 2, weeks 4–6: move everyone and drain Monero.** Point all balance accounts' CPU at Kryptex. Remove the MoneroOcean GPU route for balance accounts, and add Xelis, Iron Fish, Conflux and Tari-C29 to the Kryptex coin set so the policy can pick them when profitable. Stop assigning subaddresses. Once MoneroOcean's final payout lands, swap that XMR to BTC once and retire balance-mode Monero. *Exit test:* the bitcoind treasury is the only operator wallet, and the admin summary shows one revenue source per stream.

**Phase 3, weeks 6–8: wire the failover.** Add the unMineable adapter and an automatic route flip on Kryptex stratum or API failure, and rehearse it with a forced outage on a test machine. *Exit test:* a simulated Kryptex outage moves CPU and Pearl workers to unMineable, credits continue, and BTC arrives in the treasury without intervention.

**Phase 4, from week 8: Mysterium.** Proceed only after MystNodes answers on partner terms and beneficiary setup. Start with B2B-only traffic in a limited set of countries, credit on conversion, and cap fleet size against MYST liquidity.

## Concentration buys simplicity at the price of five correlated risks

Putting every hash on Kryptex makes one company the counterparty for all mining revenue, and its risks arrive together. **Policy risk** is already visible. Pearl went from a 1% PROP or SOLO fee in Kryptex's June 2026 article to **PPS+ 3%** on the live API in October, with no notice to miners ([Kryptex Pearl pools](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/pearl-mining-pools-en); [PRL pool info](https://pool.kryptex.com/prl/api/v1/pool/info)). **Counterparty risk** comes from terms that allow discontinuation without notice and exclude liability for "any permanent or temporary cessation", from account suspension after six months of inactivity and deletion after a year, and from pool-wallet balances deleted after 90 days ([Kryptex terms](https://www.kryptex.com/terms-and-conditions); [pool coin retention](https://pool.kryptex.com/ko/articles/how-long-pool-keep-coins-en)). **API risk** is unusually sharp, because Otium's crediting reads Kryptex's stats directly; a breaking change stops crediting, not just reporting. **Payout risk** follows from the custodial balance and email-gated withdrawals. ViaBTC's document demands on some users show that a pool can impose KYC after the fact ([ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/news/viabtc-clarifies-account-access-restrictions)). **Network-concentration risk** is the least obvious. Kryptex holds about **47% of Pearl's, 56% of Quantus's and 70% of Xelis's network hashrate** ([Kryptex PRL pool info](https://pool.kryptex.com/prl/api/v1/pool/info); [XEL pool info](https://pool.kryptex.com/xel/api/v1/pool/info)). A Kryptex incident would hit Otium's best coins at the same moment those coins themselves wobble.

The mitigations follow from the setup above. Frequent sweeps cap custodial exposure, and regular activity keeps the account clear of the inactivity rules. Server-side routes with a pre-wired unMineable failover keep CPU and Pearl paying BTC without an app release, though Quantus has no BTC-paying fallback and would stop. A weekly reconciliation of credited value against swept BTC catches silent fee or spread changes like Pearl's. A per-source haircut sized from that reconciliation, larger for PROP coins than PPS+, protects the treasury in the meantime. Own-wallet mode on MoneroOcean keeps a working non-custodial product if Kryptex ends the relationship. The steelman for staying split is real: keeping CPU on unMineable from day one would diversify by default and remove one manual sweep. It loses because it doubles the integrations to maintain, gives up PPS+ and 10-minute stats on the CPU side, and adds a fee layer, all to protect a CPU revenue stream that is small next to Pearl and Quantus.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Otium's problem. The difficulty was never mining or paying out; it was the currency in between. Monero was the only operator revenue that could not reach the Bitcoin treasury without a fragile swap, and the cheapest fix is to stop earning it rather than to get better at converting it. That makes a single conversion-capable pool the backbone, and Kryptex is the only one left that is large, PPS-based and already integrated. The automation that remains out of reach, a withdrawal from Kryptex and a sale of MYST, sits outside Otium's code: one is a vendor feature not yet offered, and the other is a market too thin to trade blind.

The same research shows how much Otium now leans on a direct competitor. Kryptex has already built Otium's product, with a desktop app, BTC payouts, no KYC and even the Mysterium bundle, and it holds most of the hashrate on the coins that pay. Otium's lasting advantage therefore cannot be the pool. It has to be what sits around the pool: macOS and Linux reach where Kryptex's app and its Mysterium bundle are Windows-only ([Kryptex Miner](https://www.kryptex.com/pro-app); [Kryptex getting started](https://www.kryptex.com/en/articles/mysterium-getting-started-en)), plus Otium's electricity-aware scheduling and per-user transparency. The failover route and own-wallet mode are what keep that advantage from depending on Kryptex's goodwill.
