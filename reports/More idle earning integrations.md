# Retune the GPU miner before chasing networks

The biggest earnings gain open to Otium in October 2026 is not a new network. It is a rewrite of the GPU profit-switching table it already has. Every legacy algorithm Otium switches between today (KawPow/RVN, Autolykos2/ERG, etchash/ETC) loses money on an RTX 4090 at $0.10/kWh. Two new algorithms that SRBMiner already supports change that. Pearl (`pearlhash`) pays about **$9.48/day profit on an RTX 4090, $2.91 on an RTX 4070 and $1.14 on an RTX 3060**. Quantus (`quantus`) pays about **$5.18/day on a 4090**. Kryptex Pool takes both at a 1% fee and auto-exchanges them to BTC/USDT, which fits Otium's operator-wallet, per-worker model directly ([WhatToMine Pearl](https://whattomine.com/coins/469-prl-pearl/gpus); [WhatToMine RTX 4090](https://whattomine.com/gpus/79-nvidia-geforce-rtx-4090); [Kryptex Pearl pools](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/pearl-mining-pools-en)). Pearl is time-limited, though: its developers plan an upgrade that will make consumer GPUs "pretty much useless". Beyond mining, only one new category passes the test of operator aggregation, in-app integration and USD crediting. That category is **B2B bandwidth**: Mysterium's partner SDK, which Kryptex already embeds in a Windows app, plus a second per-device proxy SDK such as Massive. Both pay low single-digit dollars per device per month at best, and both add antivirus and abuse risk at a time when Google and the FBI are taking down proxy networks. Everything else researched fails at least one hard test. AI inference networks either ban multiple accounts (Salad) or require personal-device attestation (Darkbloom). Storage networks forbid third-party operation (Storj) or need collateral and open ports (Sia, Swarm). Browser DePIN pays airdrop points, and BOINC/Gridcoin pays a token worth $0.003 with no trading volume. The ranked plan is: Pearl now with a kill switch, Quantus and ProgPow fallbacks behind a live profitability feed, Mysterium as a second `IBandwidthProvider`, Massive on Windows if Honeygain's contract permits it, and a Darkbloom partnership approach for Apple Silicon.

## Only mining and B2B bandwidth survive the operator test

Otium's model is narrower than "anything that pays for idle resources". The earner has to run inside Otium's own process tree. Otium has to be the paid party, aggregating many users' devices under one operator account. The money has to arrive in a form Otium can convert to USD and credit per user. Most of the October 2026 market fails this filter on terms of service or payout form rather than on technology. Salad states that "creating, or using, multiple Salad Accounts is a breach of the terms" ([Salad support](https://support.salad.com/article/46-can-i-create-multiple-accounts)). Storj's supplier terms require that the operator "own or control" each device and forbid letting nodes "be shared or used by anyone other than Node Operator" ([Storj Supplier Terms](https://www.storj.io/legal/supplier-terms-conditions)). Browser DePIN projects pay seasonal points tied to per-user wallets, so operator aggregation would look like Sybil farming. The categories that pass are the ones built for aggregation from the start: mining pools, which by design take thousands of workers under one wallet, and publisher SDKs, whose business model is a publisher monetizing many opted-in users.

The pattern is that revenue per device scales with GPU capability and falls off sharply for everything else. A mid-range NVIDIA card mining Pearl earns more in a day than a bandwidth SDK earns from that device in a year. That ratio decides the ranking at the end of this report.

## Pearl and Quantus pay 5–30x Otium's current GPU algorithms

### Pearl is the best consumer GPU earner, with an announced expiry

Pearl is an "AI proof-of-useful-work" coin that launched in April 2026. On 16 October 2026 WhatToMine ranks it first on every recent NVIDIA card at $0.10/kWh. The figures are: **RTX 4090 at 310 TH/s, $10.44 revenue and $9.48 profit per day; RTX 5080 at $5.96 profit; RTX 4070 at $2.91; RTX 3080 at $2.79; RTX 3060 at $1.14; RTX 2060 at $1.01**. GTX 16xx cards, which have no tensor cores, earn about $0.02/day ([WhatToMine Pearl GPUs](https://whattomine.com/coins/469-prl-pearl/gpus)). AMD support is disputed. Kryptex says KRig and SRBMiner run Pearl on RDNA2 and newer ([Kryptex KRig](https://www.kryptex.com/krig)), and WhatToMine lists an estimated $1.54/day for an RX 9070 XT, but one guide says AMD cannot mine it at all ([SimeonOnSecurity](https://simeononsecurity.com/articles/pearl-prl-mining-guide-2026/)). Integration cost is close to zero, because SRBMiner-Multi already implements `pearlhash`. Releases 3.7.3 to 3.7.6 (7–14 October) made new, "significantly more efficient" kernels the default on SM86/SM89/SM120 and added RTX 20/50-series support. Release 3.7.3 also recommends an 810 MHz memory clock and names errors E2001/E1027 as signs of an excessive core offset ([SRBMiner-Multi releases](https://github.com/doktor83/SRBMiner-Multi/releases)).

The risks are as large as the yield. The same SRBMiner release notes warn that "soon, Pearl will be getting an upgrade that will make consumer-grade GPUs pretty much useless", and Pearl announced a new floating-point proof-of-useful-work scheme in September with no deployment date ([SRBMiner-Multi releases](https://github.com/doktor83/SRBMiner-Multi/releases); [KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/prl-surges-300-as-pearl-gains-attention-as-ai-bitcoin)). Yields already halved once: RTX 5090 daily revenue fell from about $33.80 after launch to $17.19 by early summer, and it stands at $10.10 today ([Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/cryptomining/new-ai-compute-cryptocurrency-pearl-sparks-a-gpu-mining-rush-but-profitability-is-sliding)). An August 6 "rank-penalty softfork" cut effective hashrate by 50–87.5% for miners ranked above 128. Its mechanics are not fully described, and that matters to any operator aggregating thousands of GPUs ([SimeonOnSecurity](https://simeononsecurity.com/articles/pearl-prl-mining-guide-2026/)). The token itself is fragile. PRL rose from an all-time low of $0.14 in July to $1.40–1.90 in October. In September, SafeTrade, an exchange with no CoinGecko trust score and reported order-book depth of $0.00, carried 95% of spot volume ([KuCoin News](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/prl-surges-300-as-pearl-gains-attention-as-ai-bitcoin); [SimeonOnSecurity](https://simeononsecurity.com/articles/pearl-prl-mining-guide-2026/)). Price feeds also need care: "PRL" on KuCoin, MEXC, Gate and Coinbase is *Perle*, an unrelated token ([Coinranking](https://coinranking.com/coin/urULyL_US+perle-prl/exchanges)).

Kryptex Pool removes the liquidity problem for an operator. It charges a flat 1% on PROP or SOLO and is the only Pearl pool in its own comparison that **auto-exchanges to BTC, USDT, ETH or LTC**. It uses `WALLET.WORKER` naming, which becomes the Kryptex username when auto-exchange is on. It publishes ready SRBMiner configs, with stratum at `prl.kryptex.network:7048` (SSL 8048) plus EU and US regional hosts ([Kryptex Pearl pools](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/pearl-mining-pools-en)). Kryptex Pool also says it requires no KYC ([Kryptex](https://www.kryptex.com/en/articles/nicehash-alternative-en)). Kryptex therefore carries the conversion risk, and Otium credits USD on stable coins it actually received, not on PRL it accrued. Two facts are still unverified: that Kryptex's terms permit one account to aggregate a consumer app's whole install base, and that it exposes per-worker hashrate history through an API. Kryptex is also a direct competitor, since it runs the same kind of consumer mining app. Otium is therefore depending on a rival's pool.

### Quantus and ProgPow are the floor Otium falls back to

Quantus is a post-quantum Substrate chain (Poseidon2 proof-of-work, 12-second blocks) that launched mainnet on 9 September 2026 ([Suprnova Quantus](https://quantus.suprnova.cc/); [bytwork](https://bytwork.com/en/articles/quantus)). It ranks second on the RTX 4090 at **880 MH/s, $5.83 revenue and $5.18 profit per day** ([WhatToMine RTX 4090](https://whattomine.com/gpus/79-nvidia-geforce-rtx-4090)). Estimates from mid-September put an RTX 3070 at $1.0–1.2/day and an RTX 4070 Ti near $3/day. SRBMiner added `quantus` at 2.5% and cut it to 2%. Releases 3.6.8–3.7.6 extended it to AMD and Intel Arc, and 3.7.3 removed the QUIC transport some Quantus pools had used ([bytwork](https://bytwork.com/en/articles/quantus); [SRBMiner-Multi releases](https://github.com/doktor83/SRBMiner-Multi/releases)). Kryptex auto-exchanges Quantus too. Liquidity is worse than Pearl's: at launch the only exchange was SafeTrade, at $16–35k daily volume, and deposits under 0.1 coin were lost ([bytwork](https://bytwork.com/en/articles/quantus)). Tickers are inconsistent across sources (QTC, QUANTUS, QUAN), and WhatToMine lists an unrelated Qubitcoin as "QTC". Otium's coin IDs should key on the WhatToMine coin page, not the ticker ([WhatToMine RTX 4090](https://whattomine.com/gpus/79-nvidia-geforce-rtx-4090)).

Below those two, the same 4090 page shows how far the legacy table has decayed. **EPIC-ProgPow earns $1.24/day profit, Qubitcoin $1.22, Decred-Blake3 $0.62, Parallax $0.56, Evrmore $0.35, Zano $0.08 and Neoxa-KawPow $0.07.** Meanwhile **Ravencoin is at −$0.45, Ergo −$0.11, ETC −$0.56, Kaspa −$0.58 and Alephium −$0.84**, and Kaspa and Alephium earn $0.00 in revenue because ASICs dominate them ([WhatToMine RTX 4090](https://whattomine.com/gpus/79-nvidia-geforce-rtx-4090)). On a GTX 1050 Ti only EPIC-ProgPow and Zclassic clear a cent a day. The same comparison is worth making against the AI-rental alternative Otium cannot aggregate anyway. Salad's posted host rates from 12 September pay an RTX 4090 $0.112–0.198/hour, or $81–143/month at 100% utilization ([Salad blog](https://blog.salad.com/earning-rate-changes-september-2026/)). Pearl beats that today and Quantus roughly matches it, with no utilization risk.

### The CPU side needs guardrails, not a new coin

RandomX alternatives sit close to Monero. On WhatToMine's 16 October CPU snapshot (21 kH/s, 140 W, $0.10/kWh), **EPIC-RandomX pays $0.99/day revenue, XDAG $0.96 and Monero $0.87**. Standalone Tari-RandomX pays less at $0.77, NiceHash-RandomX pays $0.81, about 7% below direct XMR, and Tari merge mining adds only $0.02/day ([WhatToMine CPU](https://whattomine.com/cpu)). The 10–14% paper premium for EPIC/XDAG does not justify a second CPU stack whose pool liquidity and APIs are unverified. Tari merge mining would require running P2Pool and a Minotari node. Its current release halts mining until the Tari node syncs, for a 2% uplift ([P2Pool Tari issue](https://github.com/p2pool-starter-stack/pithead/issues/3094)). Qubic, which once claimed per-CPU earnings well above Monero, announced in April 2026 that its Monero mining would phase out in favour of DOGE on ASICs ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/press-releases/396050/)). An independent analysis found it earned below honest-mining revenue in any case ([arXiv 2512.01437](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.01437v2)). On Apple Silicon no credible Metal miner exists. Kryptex lists an M4 Pro at 6.82 kH/s RandomX ([Kryptex M4 Pro](https://pool.kryptex.com/device/cpu/apple/apple-m4-pro)), which by the XMR rate above grosses about $0.28/day. XMRig stays the Mac earner unless a Darkbloom deal materializes.

One caveat on terminology: the MoneroOcean fee is variously reported as 0%, 0–0.6% and 1%. A site at "moneroocean.io/fees" with a per-kH/s price table does not appear to be the genuine pool, whose domain is moneroocean.stream ([Coin Bureau](https://coinbureau.com/mining/best-monero-pools); [Monerica](https://blog.monerica.com/articles/best-monero-mining-pool)). Otium's configs should be pinned to the real endpoint.

## Bandwidth offers one real operator addition, at single-digit dollars

### Mysterium is the only node network with an operator precedent

Mysterium is the closest match to Otium's model in the whole survey, because a Windows mining app already does exactly what Otium would do. Kryptex ships MystNodes inside its desktop app behind a "Network Card" switch in PRO mode. Rewards accrue in MYST, and Kryptex converts them to BTC once they reach 5 MYST. Kryptex warns users that they act "as an exit node for others", and the feature is unavailable in some regions ([Kryptex x Mysterium](https://www.kryptex.com/en/news/kryptex_x_mysterium)). Mysterium formalizes the partner role through `mysteriumnetwork/mystnodes-sdk-client`, which "connects your application or service to the Mysterium SDK so you can monetize through bandwidth sharing". It authenticates with a `--user.token` "obtained from mystnodes team", which means onboarding is a business conversation, not self-serve ([Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/mysteriumnetwork/mystnodes-sdk-client)). The underlying node is GPL-3.0 Go for Windows, macOS and Linux. It exposes a REST control API ("Tequila"), starts with `service --agreed-terms-and-conditions`, and its auto-updater can be disabled with `MYST_UPDATER_ENABLED=false`. The Docker form needs `NET_ADMIN` and host networking ([GitHub mysteriumnetwork/node](https://github.com/mysteriumnetwork/node)). A native consumer install therefore needs a TUN/WireGuard-class driver and one-time elevation.

The economics are modest and noisy. MystNodes' own September 2026 figures are **$0.04–0.30 per GB, $2–8/month for a casual laptop and $8–25 for an always-on home node**, all labelled illustrative ([MystNodes blog](https://www.mystnodes.com/blog/how-much-can-you-earn-sharing-bandwidth)). Settlement deducts a fixed **20% network fee** plus gas, auto-triggered at 5 MYST ([Mysterium FAQ](https://docs.mysterium.network/faq)). Kryptex refuses to forecast income at all ([Kryptex x Mysterium](https://www.kryptex.com/en/news/kryptex_x_mysterium)), and an independent ROI tracker found a small setup slightly negative ([gamefi-roi Mysterium](https://gamefi-roi-web.onrender.com/opportunities/mysterium-network-node)). The legal exposure differs in kind from Honeygain's. Mysterium says it "cannot say it's 100% safe". It advises node runners in the US, UK, Canada, Germany, Italy, Australia and India not to enable "Public" traffic, and it recommends B2B-only service types ([Mysterium FAQ](https://docs.mysterium.network/faq); [MystNodes help](https://help.mystnodes.com/en/articles/8006125-node-ui-management)). Several things are still unknown and have to come from the MystNodes partner team: the partner revenue terms, whether per-device earnings are reported per node identity under one token, KYC, and the ToS on many identities sharing one beneficiary.

### Per-device proxy SDKs pay cents, and Honeygain's contract decides whether to stack one

The publisher-SDK category is alive and explicitly built for aggregation, but it pays per opted-in device rather than per unit of work, so desktop yields are cents. Proxyway's estimates are **$100–400 per 1,000 DAU per month for Honeygain and $100–350 per 1,000 IPs for Bright SDK**, in top geographies ([Proxyway](https://proxyway.com/research/internet-sharing-sdks-app-monetization)). Massive's FAQ puts its rate at $0.05–0.20 per user per month ([Massive FAQ](https://www.joinmassive.com/faq)). Its 2025 case studies (more than $1M/year from a US Android app with 100K+ DAU) imply higher rates, but they come from mobile and smart TV, not desktop ([Massive: Get Paid](https://www.joinmassive.com/getpaid)). Cash Raven caps at "up to $0.50 per user per month" ([Cash Raven](https://cashraven.io/blog/passive-income-sdks-compared)). Massive is the most practical second SDK for Otium. It documents a Windows C API callable via P/Invoke, onboards self-serve, pays monthly by PayPal or wire, ships a drop-in consent UI with an audit trail, and claims AppEsteem certification. It does not support Linux, and its macOS support is unclear ([Massive: Get Paid](https://www.joinmassive.com/getpaid)). Bright SDK pays a "fixed daily rate" and claims AV whitelisting ([bright-sdk.com](https://bright-sdk.com/)), but it says it currently wants Windows and Android partners only ([Bright Data blog](https://brightdata.com/blog/why-brightdata/bright-data-network-sdk)). It has also been the subject of 2026 consent research. Pawns is a poor fit, because its SDK terms treat traffic from idle devices without "active personal use" as invalid. That clause conflicts with Otium's premise ([Pawns SDK Terms](https://business.pawns.app/sdk-terms/)). Castar SDK and PacketSDK should not be touched: Google's threat-intelligence group tied them to the IPIDEA network it disrupted in January ([Google GTIG](https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/disrupting-largest-residential-proxy-network)).

The unresolved question is stacking. Honeygain's public partner terms require use "solely in connection with Honeygain Services" and contain no visible ban on other proxy SDKs ([Honeygain SDK partner ToS](https://sdk.honeygain.com/tos-for-partners/)). The signed partnership agreement may still say otherwise, and two proxy pools on one residential IP doubles that IP's abuse exposure. One angle is worth raising with both vendors. Otium's Honeygain integration pays only one device per IP, so a second or third PC in the same household currently earns nothing. A Massive or Mysterium node on those otherwise idle devices would add revenue without competing with Honeygain on the same machine, if the vendors' deduplication rules allow it.

### Enforcement makes every extra node a reputational bet

The enforcement climate has tightened. In January 2026 Google sued IPIDEA, seized its domains and made Play Protect block its SDKs. In July Google, the FBI and Lumen took down NetNut/Popa, cutting off about 2 million devices, and Play Protect now disables apps that contain NetNut SDKs ([BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/google-disrupts-ipidea-residential-proxy-networks-fueled-by-malware/); [BleepingComputer](https://bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/netnut-proxy-network-disrupted-2-million-infected-devices-cut-off)). Microsoft Defender already ships `PUA:Win32/RepocketProxyware` ([Microsoft Security Intelligence](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/wdsi/threats/malware-encyclopedia-description?Name=PUA%3AWin32%2FRepocketProxyware)). Microsoft's criteria, updated 10 September 2026, list "Cryptomining software (Enterprise only)" and "bundling software" as PUA categories. They require prominent notice, a clear indication while the software is active, and standard uninstall ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/unified-secops/criteria)). Security researchers keep documenting campaigns that drop miners and proxyware together, for example AhnLab's Larva-25012 ([ASEC](https://asec.ahnlab.com/en/95516/)). For an app that already ships XMRig, SRBMiner and Honeygain, each additional proxy component raises the chance that heuristics classify Otium as the very bundle they hunt. Integrations should therefore be few, separately opted into, visibly active, and carried by an AppEsteem-style certification for Otium itself.

## AI, storage and browser DePIN fail on terms, ports or points

### AI networks that pay cash will not let an operator aggregate

The AI-compute networks that pay real money are exactly the ones whose rules block Otium's model. **Darkbloom** (Eigen Labs) pays Apple Silicon Macs in USD through Stripe Connect, needs only outbound connectivity, and charges providers no platform fee ([Darkbloom provider overview](https://eigenlabs-f49d2e97.mintlify.app/provider/overview)). Its terms require providers to "operate only hardware, networks, and accounts you are authorized to use" and to keep credentials confidential. Login is an interactive device-code flow, and paid serving requires MDM enrollment that grants "administrative control" ([Darkbloom Terms](https://www.darkbloom.ai/terms.html); [Darkbloom quickstart](https://docs.darkbloom.dev/quickstart-provider)). The terms do say that a separate written agreement overrides them, and that is the only door open to Otium. The payoff behind that door is small. An independent test of an M1 Max 64 GB measured **$0.60/day, about $16/month, almost all of it a base reward** that requires at least 90% uptime. Serving any catalog model needs at least 36 GB of unified memory, the company calculator's default fell from $75/month to $12.51/month, and observers say "supply … has completely outrun demand" ([Miya AI Lab](https://miyagadget.page/en/blog/2026/06/24/darkbloom-provider-mac-earnings-review-en/); [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/dark-bloom-distributed-ai-inference)). The 90% uptime rule also conflicts with pausing whenever the user is active.

**Salad** is the only consumer-grade Windows GPU network with posted USD rates and documented game detection, but its multi-account ban and the absence of any white-label supply SDK rule it out ([Salad support](https://support.salad.com/article/46-can-i-create-multiple-accounts); [Salad docs](https://docs.salad.com/tutorials/high-performance-apps)). The token networks are wallet-based, so pooling is technically possible, but they bring stake, Linux and verification requirements. io.net requires at least 200 IO staked per GPU with a 14-day unstake ([io.net staking](https://io.net/docs/guides/staking/staking)). Nosana hosting "runs on native Linux" and requires verification plus 85% daily uptime for its incentive ([Nosana](https://nosana.com/blog/nosana-monthly-september-2026/)). Bittensor miners face a $500–5,000 startup cost and are deregistered when they fall below threshold ([misar.blog](https://www.misar.blog/@synor/articles/bittensor-subnet-mining)). Inference.net, Psyche, Dria, Spheron Fizz, Fortytwo and Lilypad pay points or nothing liquid, and Inference.net's devnet docs now redirect to its X account. Gensyn's $AI token launched in April 2026, but no per-node reward rates were found ([CoinGabbar](https://www.coingabbar.com/en/crypto-currency-news/gensyn-ai-token-launch-delphi-mainnet-kraken-2026)). There is also no hidden compute-SDK route: no publisher SDK that pays for idle CPU/GPU *compute* could be verified in October 2026. Massive mentions "running simulations" but publishes no compute rate or desktop compute build ([Massive KB](https://docs.joinmassive.com/kb/sdk/how-does-the-massive-sdk-work-and-make-money-for-developers)). Charity Engine sells compute to buyers and pays volunteers in prize-draw entries ([Charity Engine](https://www.charityengine.com/volunteer)).

### Storage pays dollars for terabytes and forbids the operator model

Storage nodes look attractive on paper, since most users have idle disk. They fail on every axis in practice. Storj cut payouts again on 1 September 2026 to **$1.35/TB-month for storage and $1.00/TB for egress**. It now pays only USDC on Ethereum L1, with thresholds around $50–70 driven by gas costs. It withholds 75% to 25% of earnings for the first nine months, demands 99.3% monthly uptime, and needs an inbound port, with "no way to skip port forwarding" behind NAT ([Storj payouts](https://storj.dev/node/payouts); [Storj prerequisites](https://storj.dev/node/get-started/prerequisites); [Storj forum](https://forum.storj.io/t/configuring-node-behind-cg-nat/24774)). Above all, its terms forbid an operator running nodes on devices it does not "own or control" ([Storj Supplier Terms](https://www.storj.io/legal/supplier-terms-conditions)). Sia hosts must lock their own Siacoin as collateral (twice the storage price by default) on a network using only about 2.08 PB of 7.30 PB advertised ([Sia docs](https://docs.sia.tech/provide-storage/configuring-your-host); [yellow.com](https://yellow.com/asset/siacoin)). Swarm requires a non-refundable 10 xBZZ stake per node and a forwarded port. Average rewards per staking node fell from 19.40 xBZZ/month in November 2025 to **6.78 xBZZ in April 2026** ([Swarm State of the Network](https://blog.ethswarm.org/foundation/2026/swarm-state-of-the-network-april-2026); [Bee FAQ](https://docs.ethswarm.org/docs/bee/bee-faq)). Filecoin Saturn is no longer active, and Internxt archived its paid node app in 2022 ([Filecoin docs](https://docs.filecoin.io/reference/general/legacy-content); [GitHub internxt/core-gui](https://github.com/internxt/core-gui)). By my calculation from the Storj rates and fill reports, a typical home node earns $0–3/month in its first year, most of it held back. That is less than the HDD's own electricity.

### Points, airdrops and science credits are not money

Browser and desktop DePIN projects pay seasonal points in tokens whose value collapses quickly. BLESS listed on six major exchanges in September 2025 and traded near $0.0055 five months later ([CoinGecko BLESS](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/bless)). Grass is the commercial standout, projecting $65–75M of 2026 data revenue, and it paid Season 2 in USDC. It offers no partner SDK, and its earnings scale with distinct public IPs ([Grass recap](https://www.grass.io/learn/july-7-grass-token-holder-and-network-participant-call/); [ownyourmind](https://ownyourmind.ai/use/how-to-earn-with-grass/)). Titan's PC rewards are points on a test network and fall as more nodes share an IP, Blockcast runs on Docker for points and NFT licences, and Meson/GaGaNode shut down in 2026 ([Titan docs](https://titannet.gitbook.io/titan-network-en/resource-network-test/operate-nodes/l2-edge-node/reward-rules/basic-rewards); [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/469158-49)). BOINC with Gridcoin is the one volunteer route an operator could technically aggregate. Gridcoin pools crunch many machines under one BOINC account ([Gridcoin wiki](https://gridcoin.us/wiki/pools)). But GRC traded at about **$0.0031 with $0 reported 24-hour volume** in July 2026 ([CoinLore](https://coinlore.com/coin/gridcoin)). Even a whole pool account earning 111 GRC/day would gross about $10/month. Folding@home pays nothing, and the merge-folding coins have no evidence of life after 2014. These have value only as a non-revenue "donate to science" mode that might help Otium's reputation.

## Five integrations, ranked by dollars per engineering week

The ranking weighs expected revenue per device, the share of Otium's install base that can use it, fit with the existing abstractions, and the downside if it goes wrong. Two are configuration-level changes to the SRBMiner path, two are new `IBandwidthProvider` implementations, and one is a partnership to pursue before writing code.

| Rank | Integration | Fits into | Who earns | Expected per device / month | Gate before shipping |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pearl via Kryptex auto-exchange, behind a kill switch | SRBMiner profit-switch table | NVIDIA RTX 20+ (AMD experimental) | ~$34–285 profit while it lasts | Kryptex operator/aggregation OK in writing |
| 2 | Quantus + ProgPow fallbacks, live server-side ranking, retire loss-making algos | SRBMiner table + server config | All Windows/Linux GPUs | ~$30–155 (Quantus), $2–37 (ProgPow) | Pool routes per coin; data-API terms |
| 3 | Mysterium partner node, B2B-only | New `IBandwidthProvider` | All three OSes | ~$2–8 typical | MystNodes partner token and terms; counsel on exit-node risk |
| 4 | Massive SDK (Windows), aimed at devices Honeygain does not pay | New `IBandwidthProvider` | Windows | ~$0.05–0.50 | Honeygain exclusivity check; Massive dedup rules |
| 5 | Darkbloom operator agreement for Apple Silicon | New `IComputeProvider` (macOS) | Macs with ≥36 GB for job income | ~$0–18 | Written agreement with Eigen Labs |

### 1. Add `pearlhash` to the switching table as a remotely killable row

Add a `pearlhash` algorithm row to SRBMiner's profit-switch table with a device-eligibility predicate: NVIDIA compute capability ≥7.5, meaning RTX 20-series and newer with tensor cores. Ship AMD RDNA2+ as an opt-in experimental row, given the conflicting support claims, and exclude GTX 16xx and older. Point it at Kryptex (`prl.kryptex.network:8048` SSL, with the EU/US regional hosts chosen by latency), with the login set to Otium's Kryptex username plus `.{workerId}` and auto-exchange to USDT, so no PRL ever sits on Otium's books. Require SRBMiner ≥3.7.6 for the new kernels. Teach the watchdog that E2001/E1027 mean the core offset is too high and should be stepped down, and apply the 810 MHz memory-clock guidance only on cards where Otium already manages clocks ([SRBMiner-Multi releases](https://github.com/doktor83/SRBMiner-Multi/releases)). Gate the whole row behind a server-pushed flag, `algos.pearlhash.enabled`, plus an automatic floor: the client drops Pearl the moment its measured revenue per TH/s falls below the next-best algorithm's, so the expected overnight collapse on the consumer-killing upgrade degrades gracefully instead of mining for nothing. Server crediting follows the existing per-worker pattern. Clients report accepted shares and hashrate from SRBMiner's local API every few minutes. The server reconciles those reports against Kryptex's per-worker statistics, if the API exposes them, and against its USDT payouts. Users' balances move from "pending" to "credited" in USD only when the USDT lands, at the actual conversion. Before launch, get two answers from Kryptex in writing: whether thousands of consumer workers under one account is allowed, and how the August rank-penalty softfork treats a large pooled miner.

### 2. Make the server rank algorithms live and retire the loss-makers

Pearl will end, and the rankings above changed completely within six months, so the durable fix is architectural. The ranking table should stop being static. The server pulls revenue-per-unit-hashrate for each eligible coin from WhatToMine or hashrate.no on a schedule and pushes a signed ranking to clients, which keeps kill switches and data-provider terms in one place. Each client multiplies the ranking by its own per-device benchmark, subtracts measured watts times the user's tariff and the miner dev fee, and switches only past a hysteresis band. Add `quantus` routed through Kryptex with USDT auto-exchange. Avoid any pool endpoint that requires QUIC, since SRBMiner 3.7.3 removed it ([SRBMiner-Multi releases](https://github.com/doktor83/SRBMiner-Multi/releases)). Add EPIC-ProgPow as the main post-Pearl fallback, with Evrmore, Zano and Neoxa as marginal tiers; pool and API routes for these were not verified and need choosing. Demote KawPow, Autolykos2 and etchash to a "free power only" tier that runs solely when the user's tariff is zero, and remove Kaspa, Alephium and Nexa as GPU targets ([WhatToMine RTX 4090](https://whattomine.com/gpus/79-nvidia-geforce-rtx-4090)). The operator-model tension needs to be stated plainly in the product. Otium's revenue depends on gross revenue, while the user's welfare depends on net after electricity. Leaving a loss-making algorithm running earns Otium a cut while costing the user money, so the default should be "never mine at a net loss".

### 3. Ship Mysterium as a second, separately consented `IBandwidthProvider`

Implement a `MysteriumBandwidthProvider` that bundles the signed native node (or the partner SDK client) per OS rather than Docker, launches it with Otium's partner token, and drives it through the Tequila REST API for identity, session and earnings data. Disable the built-in updater so Otium controls versions and signatures ([GitHub mysteriumnetwork/node](https://github.com/mysteriumnetwork/node)). Lock service types to B2B and keep Public traffic off everywhere, or at minimum in the seven countries Mysterium names ([Mysterium FAQ](https://docs.mysterium.network/faq)). Put it behind its own opt-in that uses Kryptex-style exit-node wording, never folded into the Honeygain consent. Install the TUN/WireGuard component once with elevation, and keep the uninstall path standard. Credit actual per-node earnings, not sharing hours as with Honeygain, because Mysterium pays per GB and per region. The server reads each node identity's unsettled MYST, settles at 5 MYST, deducts the 20% fee, converts, and credits USD at realized value. Expect $2–8 per typical device. The case for building it rests on all-OS coverage and the Kryptex precedent, not on its size.

### 4. Add Massive on Windows where Honeygain pays nothing

Implement `MassiveBandwidthProvider` over Massive's Windows C API by P/Invoke, using its mandatory consent dialog and taskbar activity indicator. Credit it per sharing hour, as Honeygain is credited, with the monthly PayPal payout split pro rata across user-hours and shown as pending until Massive pays ([Massive: Get Paid](https://www.joinmassive.com/getpaid)). Its best use is targeted. Run it on the second and later devices behind an IP that Honeygain already pays for, and in geographies where Honeygain pays poorly, so the two never compete on one machine. Do this only after Honeygain confirms in writing that its agreement allows it and Massive confirms how it deduplicates IPs. The revenue is cents per device, so the build is justified only because the integration is small and Otium already has the crediting plumbing. If Otium's AV standing deteriorates after Mysterium ships, cut this one first.

### 5. Open a partnership conversation with Eigen Labs before writing Mac code

Darkbloom is the only cash-paying idle earner that beats XMRig on Apple Silicon, but its terms make an Otium-run fleet legitimate only through a written agreement ([Darkbloom Terms](https://www.darkbloom.ai/terms.html)). If Eigen Labs agrees, a `DarkbloomComputeProvider` would wrap the `darkbloom` CLI (`start`, `status`, `earnings`). It would run the device-code login once per Mac under Otium's account and map Otium's idle schedule onto `provider.toml` availability windows ([Darkbloom quickstart](https://docs.darkbloom.dev/quickstart-provider)). It would gate job income to Macs with at least 36 GB of unified memory and credit from the per-session earnings output. The agreement should also cover the 90% uptime rule for the base reward, which is most of the measured income, and an MDM consent flow that a third-party brand can present credibly. Without that agreement, the only compliant option is a user-owned Darkbloom account, which falls outside Otium's balance model.

### Operator crediting carries its own legal weight

Every recommendation above deepens Otium's role as the party that receives third-party revenue and owes users balances. Earlier research flagged that mining-pool operators "who host all the wallets on behalf of pool members" appear to need US money-services-business registration ([Hunton](https://huntonak.com/blockchain-legal-resource/new-fincen-guidance-on-virtual-currency)). In the EU, only authorized providers may offer crypto custody and transfer since MiCA's transition ended on 1 July 2026 ([Elvinger Hoss](https://elvingerhoss.lu/insights/publications/mica-transitional-period-has-come-end-what-comes-next-casps)). Converting to USD at the pool or SDK boundary and crediting a USD balance follows the pattern incumbents moved toward in 2026, with Salad's points and JumpTask's fixed-value credits as examples ([Honeygain](https://support.honeygain.com/hc/en-us/articles/28445831396380-What-s-changing-in-JumpTask-2026)). Paying users in crypto from that balance should still be reviewed by counsel. In the US, payments to a user above $2,000 a year trigger 1099 reporting ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-threshold.html)), which Pearl-era GPU earnings can now reach.

## What not to build, and why

| Don't build | Reason |
|---|---|
| Storj, Sia or Swarm storage nodes | Storj terms forbid operating nodes on devices Otium does not own or control. Sia and Swarm need per-node collateral or stake plus port forwarding. Yields are $0–4/month. |
| Salad integration | Multiple accounts are banned and there is no supply-side SDK. |
| io.net, Nosana, Akash, Golem, Bittensor workers | Linux/Docker, per-GPU stake or verification, aggregation terms unverified, and no measured consumer ROI. |
| Grass, Bless, Gradient, Nodepay, Teneo, Dawn, Titan, Blockcast | Points and airdrop tokens, no operator SDK, and Sybil rules. |
| BOINC/Gridcoin as revenue | GRC is about $0.003 with ~$0 volume and cannot fund USD credits. Offer only as an unpaid science mode, if at all. |
| Theta Edge Node | Heavy hardware, TFUEL ~$0.013, ROI rated "not measurable", and no partner model ([gamefi-roi](https://gamefi-roi-web.onrender.com/opportunities/theta-edge-node)). |
| Castar, PacketSDK and other IPIDEA-family SDKs | Tied to the network Google disrupted, and Play Protect blocks them. |
| Pawns SDK | Idle-device traffic is invalid under its terms. |
| Qubic, Tari merge mining, EPIC/XDAG CPU switching | Qubic is exiting Monero. Tari adds ~2% for a node stack. CPU alternatives add ~10% on paper with unverified pools. |
| Kaspa, Alephium, Nexa, and KawPow/ETC/Ergo at paid tariffs | ASIC-dominated or loss-making on a 4090 at $0.10/kWh. |
| Metal GPU mining on Macs | No credible miner exists, and Pearl/Quantus miners target CUDA/ROCm/OpenCL only. |
| NiceHash as a backend | Its RandomX price is ~7% below direct XMR ([WhatToMine CPU](https://whattomine.com/cpu)), it requires KYC, and Excavator cannot be redistributed. |

## Conclusion

Otium's existing architecture is better placed than its current configuration suggests. The provider abstractions, the per-worker crediting and the operator wallet already fit the only opportunities that pay real money under an operator model. The weak point is that the GPU table is pointed at algorithms that now cost users more in power than they earn. Fixing that is worth more than every new network in this report combined. It also shows that the durable asset is a server-side ranking engine with kill switches, not any particular coin, because the most profitable algorithm of October 2026 did not exist in April and is already scheduled to shut consumers out.

The second lesson is that the idle-resource market has split along a line that matters for Otium's legal and reputational exposure. Compute that pays cash is gated behind personal-device attestation or bans on multiple accounts, and bandwidth that welcomes operators is under active law-enforcement pressure. Each bandwidth addition therefore buys a few dollars per device at the price of more AV and abuse surface. Otium should add Mysterium for its cross-OS reach, be selective about anything further, and treat consent quality and vendor reputation as product features. The open items are mostly emails, not engineering: Kryptex on operator aggregation, MystNodes on partner terms, Honeygain on exclusivity, and Eigen Labs on a fleet agreement.
