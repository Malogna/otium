# Consumer "earn with your idle PC" apps: competitors, architecture, legal/tax and electricity (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: research done 2026-10-15 with about 15 searches. Much of it comes from search-engine summaries of the pages listed. Primary pages were not fetched one by one, so check figures marked "verify" before relying on them. Stale items are flagged inline.

## 1. Existing aggregator and consumer apps (competitive landscape)

### Takeaway
The field is split three ways. (a) One-click consumer apps that hide the backend and pay in fiat, gift cards or BTC: Salad, Kryptex, NiceHash QuickMiner. (b) Rig-management tools for hobbyist farms, billed per rig per month: Hive OS, minerstat, Awesome Miner. (c) Bandwidth-sharing "passive income" apps: Honeygain/JumpTask, Grass, Nodepay. Every consumer compute app faces the same three complaints: low payouts after electricity, unpredictable demand, and antivirus flags. Cudo, one of the earlier multi-backend players, has effectively left the consumer market.

### Cited Findings

**Salad (Windows desktop app, "chopping")**
- Salad runs container workloads (mostly AI inference for SaladCloud customers) inside WSL2 on Windows. The desktop client uses Hyper-V virtualization and boots real Linux kernels via WSL2 — [SaladCloud SCE docs](https://docs.salad.com/products/sce/introduction); [Salad community: Container Workloads and Your PC](https://community.salad.com/container-workloads-and-your-pc/)
- Only Linux AMD64 containers are supported. Windows containers and ARM are not supported. Virtualization must be enabled in BIOS. Salad cannot run behind a VPN or proxy — [Salad Support: container workloads troubleshooting](https://support.salad.com/article/346-container-workloads-troubleshooting); [GitHub salad-kitchen-docs troubleshooting](https://github.com/SaladTechnologies/salad-kitchen-docs/blob/main/content/docs/troubleshooting/container-jobs/container-workloads-troubleshooting.md)
- Hardware requirements for container work: recent NVIDIA GPUs only, 8 GB+ VRAM recommended, at least 8 GB RAM, 70 GB free disk, 15 Mbps up and down — [Salad GitHub FAQ](https://github.com/SaladTechnologies/salad-kitchen-docs/blob/main/content/docs/faq/compatibility/is-my-machine-compatible-with-salad.md). Salad's troubleshooting page says "around 100GB" of storage, and a third-party guide says 16 GB RAM and 50 GB disk, so the official sources are inconsistent — [ggCircuit guide](https://helpdesk.ggcircuit.com/en/article/getting-started-with-salad-system-requirements-setup-guide-1i7r1kw/)
- Anti-cheat in some games conflicts with virtualization being turned on, so users may need to disable it in BIOS to play those games — [Salad community](https://community.salad.com/container-workloads-and-your-pc/)
- Payout model: earnings build up as "Salad Balance" or reward points with no cash value. They are spent in the Salad Storefront on PayPal transfers, prepaid cards and gift cards, delivered to a "Reward Vault" or by email — [Salad Terms for Chefs](https://salad.com/terms/chefs/); [Salad Support: chopping](https://support.salad.com/article/333-chopping-power). This "store credit / rewards" structure is legally significant (see section 5).
- Salad's own explanation of antivirus flags: "this is just a false positive… detecting the miner library plugins we use." It tells users to whitelist Salad — [Salad Support: Is Salad a virus?](https://support.salad.com/article/205-is-salad-a-virus)
- Reputation in 2026, from Trustpilot user reviews (anecdotal): praised for easy setup and fast cashout. Complaints include earnings often below electricity cost, volatile job demand, advertised rates higher than actual, and some cancelled withdrawals. Salad replies by pointing users to its demand monitor — [Trustpilot Salad p1](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/salad.com); [p3](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/salad.com?page=3); [p6](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/salad.com?page=6)
- Third-party claims, unverified: a platform fee of about 20–25% and up to about $0.60/hr for an RTX 4090 — [CloudEarn Salad review 2026](https://www.cloudearn.net/salad/). PaidFromSurveys calls it legit but "not worth it" for most people. That site earns referral income, so it is biased — [PaidFromSurveys](https://paidfromsurveys.com/salad-com-review)

**Kryptex (Windows miner app plus Kryptex Pool)**
- The app pays in BTC or fiat. Withdrawals are converted by a partner exchange at a rate "slightly lower than average", so the exchange spread is the hidden fee. Kryptex markets withdrawals as fee-free — [Kryptex Fees](https://www.kryptex.com/en/fees); [Kryptex vs Kryptex Pool](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/kryptex-vs-kryptex-pool-en)
- USD payouts go through Volet at 1.95%, with a $1 minimum and $1,000 maximum — [Kryptex Fees](https://www.kryptex.com/en/fees). There is no direct PayPal option in the app. Kryptex's guide says to send BTC/ETH/LTC to the user's PayPal crypto wallet and says "PayPal USD will be added soon". That article is about 5–6 months old, so it may be stale — [Kryptex PayPal guide](https://www.kryptex.com/en/articles/kryptex-paypal-en). Kryptex Pool docs list PayPal US as a payout option, which conflicts with the guide — [Kryptex account mining](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/account-mining-en)
- Kryptex Pool: 1% pool fee, hourly payouts with no network fee, paid in the coin mined. Its "account mining" product lets you mine anything and get BTC — [Kryptex Pool](https://pool.kryptex.com/articles/about-pool-en)
- On antivirus: Kryptex says AVs mark it "Not-A-Virus"/PUA. It advises users to disable PUA blocking in Windows reputation-based protection and add folder exclusions. That weakens the user's security and is a pattern to avoid — [Kryptex: not a virus](https://www.kryptex.com/de/articles/not-a-virus-en)
- A Kryptex app also appears on the macOS/iOS store listings aggregator — [mwm.ai listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/kryptex/6476491438). Apple's rules mean it can only be a monitoring or account app, not an on-device miner (see section 5). Verify.

**NiceHash (QuickMiner, NiceHash Miner, EasyMining)**
- NiceHash is a two-sided hashpower marketplace. Sellers (miners) pay about 2% on payouts. Buyers pay about 3% of spend plus 0.00001 BTC per order. The internal-wallet payout threshold is typically 0.001 BTC. Accounts inactive for more than 6 months are charged inactivity fees. These figures come from 2025–2026 secondary reviews, and trading-fee tiers conflict (0.5%/0.4% vs about 0.2%) — [CoinSpot NiceHash review 2025](https://coinspot.io/en/technology/nicehash-review-and-mining-marketplace-overview/); [CryptoNinjas 2026](https://www.cryptoninjas.net/crypto/nicehash-review/); [Plisio 2026](https://plisio.net/profiles/what-is-nicehash)
- QuickMiner runs only NiceHash's own Excavator plus open-source XMRig, so NiceHash expects it "to work without AV issues". NiceHash Miner runs third-party miners whose contents NiceHash says it doesn't know, and NiceHash has said it cares less about AV detections there — [NiceHash QuickMiner GitHub issue #204](https://github.com/nicehash/NiceHashQuickMiner/issues/204). An older claim says QuickMiner has no developer fee versus 2–3% for NiceHash Miner. Stale, verify — [Coin Bureau guide](https://coinbureau.com/guides/nicehash-profits-beginners)
- Windows Defender reputation-based protection blocks NiceHash binaries. "Allow on device" sometimes doesn't stick, and folder exclusions are the user workaround — [NiceHash QuickMiner GitHub issue #204](https://github.com/nicehash/NiceHashQuickMiner/issues/204); [Microsoft Answers PUA:Win32/CoinMiner](https://answers.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/forum/all/windows-defender-puawin32coinminer-active-false/e2f3213a-a8f6-4a0d-b357-9097bff65548)
- EasyMining is a "lottery" product: users buy predefined hashpower packages (example 0.0021 BTC, about €201) and keep the full block reward if their package finds a block. NiceHash marketing (Sept 2025) claims three BTC blocks were found this way in one month — [Chainwire/Barchart Sept 2025](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/34543434/three-bitcoin-blocks-mined-last-month-with-no-hardware-via-nicehash-easymining); [NiceHash EasyMining](https://www.nicehash.com/my/easymining). Trustpilot users complain that advertised speed differs from actual pool speed and dispute payout fees — [Trustpilot NiceHash](https://ie.trustpilot.com/review/www.nicehash.com)

**Cudo Miner / CUDO Compute**
- CUDO Compute closed its public on-demand platform on 31 March 2026 and repositioned as an enterprise AI-infrastructure provider — [CUDO Compute docs](https://docs.cudocompute.com/)
- Cudo Miner, the consumer app: one download mirror says "This download is no longer available". A scam-watch site lists it as having no updates since about 2020–2022. There is no official shutdown notice, so treat it as effectively abandoned (unconfirmed) — [download.it](https://cudo-miner.en.download.it/); [BestCloudMining watch list](https://www.bestcloudmining.net/scam-watch/)

**Rig-management tools (Hive OS, minerstat, Awesome Miner)**
- Hive OS (Hiveon OS): 2 workers free. GPU rigs cost $0.50 per card per month up to $3/month for 6+ cards. CPU-only rigs cost $0.50/month. ASICs are free with Hiveon firmware or $2/month via ASIC Hub. There was a percentage-of-hashrate alternative tied to Hiveon Pool, but the Hiveon site now shows a banner saying "Hiveon Pool closes May 15". Billing figures date from March 2024 and may be stale — [Hiveon billing FAQ](https://hiveon.com/knowledge-base/faq/billing_in_hive_os/billing); [Hiveon pricing](https://hiveon.com/pricing/); [Hiveon news](https://hiveon.com/news/get-all-benefits-of-hiveon-os-for-a-percentage-of-your-hashrate/)
- minerstat: about $2 per worker per month (one worker = a rig with up to 16 GPUs or 1 ASIC), with up to 20% discount for longer terms. Source is a third-party listing — [G2/aggregator](https://www.g2.com/products/awesome-miner/pricing)
- Awesome Miner: free for up to 2 miners with limited features. Paid tiers are reported at about $4–9/month (about $2 per miner per month). Third-party sources only — [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/awesome-miner/pricing); [investing.co.uk 2025](https://investing.co.uk/awesome-miner)

**Bandwidth-sharing "multi-earn" apps (Honeygain, JumpTask, Grass, Nodepay)**
- Honeygain's minimum payout is $20 — [Honeygain support](https://support.honeygain.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412730754706-What-is-the-minimum-payout-threshold). New Honeygain terms took effect on 10 Aug 2026 — [Honeygain ToU](https://www.honeygain.com/terms-of-use/)
- JumpTask, part of Honeygain, changed in 2026. Crypto is no longer handled in-app. Balances were converted to fixed credits (1,000 credits = $1) on 10 Aug 2026. Payouts in gift cards, crypto and PayPal now go through a third-party payout partner, GIFQ, with a $0.50 minimum ($1 first payout) — [Honeygain: What's changing in JumpTask 2026](https://support.honeygain.com/hc/en-us/articles/28445831396380-What-s-changing-in-JumpTask-2026). This is a notable 2026 trend: an earn app moving away from holding crypto itself toward a payout partner and fixed-value credits.
- Grass rewards users for sharing idle bandwidth for AI web-data scraping (vendor claims) — [Grass](https://www.grass.io/learn/how-you-re-rewarded-for-helping-gather-public-web-data-for-ai/). Nodepay is a similar Chrome extension, with no 2026 status found. Fake "boosted earnings" plug-ins for these networks circulate as scams — [Medium on Nodepay](https://medium.com/@Carrsos1/nodepay-earning-10-to-15-just-like-grass-9440a9de7239); [PANews](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/afahxv56gfqm)

### Inferences
- Salad's fee is not disclosed: it is effectively the spread between what SaladCloud charges and what users receive, paid out as store credit. Kryptex's fee is the FX/exchange spread plus withdrawal-rail fees. NiceHash charges explicit percentages on both sides. A new aggregator will probably need to show its fees openly to stand out, given repeated complaints that "advertised rate > actual".
- Two models for choosing workloads exist. One is demand-driven: Salad sends whatever SaladCloud customers deploy, and users can't choose. The other is profit-switching via benchmarks: Kryptex and NiceHash benchmark algorithms locally and switch to the most profitable one. A multi-backend aggregator combines both, ranking "AI rental job available" against "best mining algo now" net of electricity.
- No major consumer app found offers true one-click setup on all three of Windows, Linux and macOS. Salad is Windows-first, Kryptex is Windows-centric, and Hive OS is a Linux distro. Full cross-platform support would set a new app apart, but macOS has little to sell: on-device mining isn't economic on Apple GPUs, and there is no CUDA.

### Gaps
- No authoritative 2026 user counts or revenue for Salad, Kryptex or NiceHash were found. Salad's fee percentage is unofficial.
- No vetted list of 2025–2026 newcomer compute aggregators was found. Searches surfaced bandwidth DePIN apps (Grass, Nodepay), not new GPU or CPU aggregators. Worth a dedicated search on io.net workers, Nosana, Akash providers, Vast.ai hosts and Clore.ai as backends, likely covered by other researchers.
- Whether Kryptex's direct PayPal USD payout is live in Oct 2026 is unconfirmed, because the sources conflict.
- NiceHash's current official fee schedule and QuickMiner developer fee were not confirmed from the primary page.

## 2. Technical architecture patterns (sandboxing, GPU, idle detection, signing, AV)

### Takeaway
The proven way to isolate untrusted third-party AI workloads on consumer Windows is Salad's: Linux containers inside WSL2/Hyper-V with NVIDIA GPU paravirtualization. It brings real costs: BIOS virtualization, about 70–100 GB of disk, anti-cheat conflicts, and no VPN or proxy. Mining binaries can't be made AV-clean, because Microsoft classes them as PUA:Win32/CoinMiner. Since 2024, EV certificates no longer skip SmartScreen, so reputation has to be built up over time.

### Cited Findings
- Sandbox: Salad runs untrusted Linux containers in WSL2 and markets WSL2 as protecting the PC by "isolating container workloads in sandboxed" environments, using Hyper-V and real Linux kernels — [Salad community](https://community.salad.com/container-workloads-and-your-pc/); [SaladCloud SCE](https://docs.salad.com/products/sce/introduction)
- Constraints of that design: virtualization must be on in BIOS, Linux/AMD64 containers only, NVIDIA only, large disk needs, and VPN/proxy incompatibility. Game anti-cheat can refuse to start while virtualization is active — [Salad troubleshooting](https://support.salad.com/article/346-container-workloads-troubleshooting); [Salad community](https://community.salad.com/container-workloads-and-your-pc/)
- AV classification: Microsoft Defender detects miners as `PUA:Win32/CoinMiner`, a "potentially unwanted" category rather than malware. Reputation-based protection blocks them unless the user allows them or adds exclusions — [Microsoft Answers](https://answers.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/forum/all/windows-defender-puawin32coinminer-active-false/e2f3213a-a8f6-4a0d-b357-9097bff65548); [NiceHash GitHub #204](https://github.com/nicehash/NiceHashQuickMiner/issues/204)
- Mitigation patterns used by vendors:
  - NiceHash QuickMiner limits itself to first-party Excavator plus open-source XMRig to reduce detections — [NiceHash GitHub #204](https://github.com/nicehash/NiceHashQuickMiner/issues/204)
  - Salad and Kryptex tell users to whitelist the app. Kryptex even advises turning off PUA blocking — [Salad](https://support.salad.com/article/205-is-salad-a-virus); [Kryptex](https://www.kryptex.com/de/articles/not-a-virus-en)
  - Detection varies by AV vendor. For example, Kaspersky did not flag a NiceHash DLL that others did — [MalwareTips](https://malwaretips.com/threads/bitdefender-and-others-detected-it-but-kaspersky-didnt-im-a-bit-concerned.135172/post-1120035)
- Code signing and SmartScreen: Microsoft's docs say EV certificates no longer bypass SmartScreen, and paying for EV just for that is "no longer justified". EV-signed files now build reputation the same way OV-signed files do. The change dates to 2024 — [Microsoft Learn: SmartScreen reputation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/smartscreen-reputation); [ToDesktop PSA](https://www.todesktop.com/blog/posts/windows-apps-psa-ev-certs-do-not-grant-immediate-reputation-anymore); [DigiCert KB](https://knowledge.digicert.com/alerts/ev-signed-application-showing-microsoft-defender-smartscreen-warnings)
- SmartScreen decisions depend on publisher and file-hash reputation. New binaries warn until reputation builds, reputation is not permanent, and Microsoft has no enrollable false-positive allowlist program — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5883575/issue-with-microsoft-smartscreen-and-digicert); [Microsoft Q&A 2](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5584097/how-to-bypass-windows-defender-smartscreen-even-af). Microsoft's code-signing options page covers Azure Artifact Signing (formerly Trusted Signing) as a cheaper signing route — [Microsoft Learn: code signing options](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/package-and-deploy/code-signing-options); [comcomponent 2026 guide](https://comcomponent.com/en/blog/2026/05/11/000-windows-smartscreen-code-signing-guide/)

### Inferences
- Recommended split: ship a small, clean, signed shell (UI, scheduler, telemetry) that never contains miner code. Download workload engines (miners, container images) on demand into a dedicated folder after explicit user opt-in. A first-run wizard should add a narrow Defender exclusion only for that folder, with consent, and never turn off PUA protection globally. That keeps the main installer's SmartScreen and AV reputation separate from the miners'.
- For AI/rental backends, rely on each backend's own isolation (WSL2 or Docker containers, as Salad, Vast.ai and others do) rather than writing a hypervisor. On Linux, use Docker plus the NVIDIA Container Toolkit natively. On macOS there is no GPU passthrough into Linux VMs for CUDA work, so macOS is realistically limited to CPU tasks or Apple-native (Metal/MLX) workloads, if any backend supports them.
- Idle detection and thermal limits (inferred, no sources gathered): use the Windows `GetLastInputInfo`, macOS `IOHIDSystem` HIDIdleTime, and X11/Wayland idle-notify APIs. Detect fullscreen apps and games to pause. NVML can set power limits on NVIDIA. Auto-start via Task Scheduler/Run key, launchd LaunchAgent and systemd user units. Updates via Velopack/Squirrel-style updaters. These are standard patterns but were not verified in this session.

### Gaps
- No source found on how Kryptex or NiceHash detect idle state or apply thermal limits. No 2026 sources on Docker Desktop vs raw WSL2 licensing for redistributed consumer apps. No details on Azure Artifact Signing eligibility or pricing in 2026.
- macOS notarization requirements for apps that download and run unsigned helper binaries (hardened runtime, `com.apple.security.cs.disable-library-validation`) were not researched here.
- No AMD GPU container support was found in Salad (NVIDIA only). AMD consumer GPU passthrough to WSL2 via ROCm was not checked.

## 3. Cross-platform UI framework fit (Avalonia on .NET 9 vs WPF vs Electron vs Tauri)

### Takeaway
Avalonia is the natural choice for a C#/.NET cross-platform tray-resident app. Its TrayIcon with NativeMenu works on Windows, macOS and "some Linux distributions (confirmed Ubuntu)", so Linux tray behavior must be tested per desktop environment. WPF is Windows-only, which rules it out for this goal.

### Cited Findings
- Avalonia TrayIcon is supported on Windows, macOS and some Linux distributions, confirmed on Ubuntu. The tray menu must be a `NativeMenu`, not the Avalonia `Menu` control. It is declared in `App.axaml` under `TrayIcon.Icons`, with the icon as an `AvaloniaResource` — [Avalonia docs: TrayIcon](https://docs.avaloniaui.net/controls/navigation/trayicon); [Avalonia v12 docs: TrayIcon](https://v12.docs.avaloniaui.net/controls/navigation/trayicon); [Avalonia docs: NativeMenu](https://docs.avaloniaui.net/controls/menus/nativemenu)
- The TrayIcon/NativeMenu API is the same across 0.10.x, 11.x and the v12 docs, so it is stable. A v12 docs site exists, meaning Avalonia 12 is current or released by 2026 — [Avalonia v12 docs](https://v12.docs.avaloniaui.net/controls/navigation/trayicon); [Avalonia v11 docs](https://v11.docs.avaloniaui.net/docs/reference/controls/tray-icon)
- NativeMenu also drives the macOS app and dock menus. The Russian docs page is narrower, which looks like a docs inconsistency — [Avalonia NativeMenu](https://docs.avaloniaui.net/controls/menus/nativemenu); [RU page](https://docs.avaloniaui.net/ru/docs/reference/controls/nativemenu)
- GPU telemetry: NVML (`nvmlDeviceGetPowerUsage`, in milliwatts) is a C API usable through P/Invoke from .NET. It covers NVIDIA board power including memory — [NVML API reference](https://docs.nvidia.com/deploy/nvml-api/latest/api/group__nvmlDeviceQueries.html)

### Inferences
- With Avalonia, one C# codebase covers UI, tray, NVML/ADLX P/Invoke and the orchestration engine. Electron and Tauri would need a separate native or sidecar process (Node or Rust) for NVML, RAPL and process control anyway, which loses the single-language advantage the user wants. Tauri gives small binaries, but its tray and webview behavior on Linux has similar desktop-environment quirks (not verified this session).
- Linux tray risk: GNOME without the AppIndicator extension often hides tray icons. Provide a fallback main window or notification and don't depend on the tray alone. This is an inference consistent with Avalonia's "some distributions" caveat.
- .NET 9 support ended (STS) in May 2026. .NET 10 LTS (Nov 2025) is the better target as of Oct 2026. This comes from general knowledge of the .NET support policy and was not verified with a source this session; flag it to the report writer.

### Gaps
- No source collected on AMD ADLX/ADL .NET bindings, Intel Arc telemetry (Level Zero/IGCL), or macOS IOKit/Metal GPU enumeration from .NET.
- No head-to-head Avalonia vs Electron vs Tauri benchmark for 2026 was gathered.

## 4. Electricity-cost accounting (power measurement, prices, net profit)

### Takeaway
Measured power is easy on NVIDIA (NVML, no root), harder for CPUs (RAPL on Linux needs root or a udev/group change since kernel 5.10, and Windows has no simple public RAPL API), and hardest on macOS (`powermetrics` needs root and gives estimates). Apps should combine measured GPU watts, estimated CPU or system watts, and a user-entered $/kWh to show net profit. User reviews show that electricity cost is the main thing that decides whether these apps are worth running.

### Cited Findings
- `nvmlDeviceGetPowerUsage` returns milliwatts for the whole board (GPU plus memory). On Ampere (except GA100) and newer it is a 1-second average; GA100 and older give instantaneous values. Fermi/Kepler accuracy is about ±5% — [NVML Device Queries](https://docs.nvidia.com/deploy/nvml-api/latest/api/group__nvmlDeviceQueries.html)
- Prefer the total-energy counter API on Volta and newer rather than polling and integrating power — [ML.ENERGY: Measuring GPU energy](https://ml.energy/blog/energy/measurement/measuring-gpu-energy-best-practices/)
- Some GPUs return `NVML_ERROR_NOT_SUPPORTED` for power, so code must handle that — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/is-nvmldevicegetpowerusage-working-with-cuda-4-0-and-c2050-do-all-the-nvml-apis-work-with-the-lat/22727)
- Linux RAPL: `/sys/class/powercap/intel-rapl*/energy_uj` has been root-only since kernel 5.10-rc4, because of the PLATYPUS side-channel (CVE-2020-8694/8695) — [Intel advisory INTEL-SA-00389](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/software-security-guidance/advisory-guidance/running-average-power-limit-energy-reporting.html); [powercap issue #7](https://github.com/powercap/powercap/issues/7). Workarounds: a dedicated group with mode 0440 via a udev rule. Note that MODE on the symlinked node doesn't work; use `RUN+=` chgrp/chmod on `/sys%p/energy_uj` — [CodeCarbon RAPL guide](https://docs.codecarbon.io/latest/how-to/enable-rapl/); [Arch forum](https://bbs.archlinux.org/viewtopic.php?id=307345); [rapl-energy](https://github.com/JordyAaldering/rapl-energy)
- macOS: `powermetrics` must run as root and reports CPU/GPU/ANE power in mW as estimates. Apple removed package power, so the parts must be summed. The practical tools (asitop and others) target Apple Silicon. Options for a non-interactive app: a narrow sudoers rule or a privileged helper — [asitop](https://github.com/tlkh/asitop); [Mole.fit](https://mole.fit/blog/how-to-check-mac-temperature); [robe.one](https://blog.robe.one/display-cpu-energy-consumption-on-apple-silicon-with-python)
- Users cite electricity as the main reason Salad isn't worth it, and Salad points users to a demand monitor — [Trustpilot Salad](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/salad.com); [PaidFromSurveys](https://paidfromsurveys.com/salad-com-review)

### Inferences
- Suggested accounting: net = gross payout − (measured GPU Wh + estimated CPU Wh from RAPL or a TDP×utilization model + idle-baseline delta) × tariff. Count only the extra power above the PC's idle draw. Ask the user for $/kWh at setup, with country defaults. Optional time-of-use schedules can come later. Use a "don't run if net < 0" toggle, as profit-switchers effectively do.
- On Windows, CPU package power without a kernel driver is hard. Tools like LibreHardwareMonitor use a signed kernel driver (WinRing0 family), which itself triggers AV flags. Prefer an estimate model on Windows CPUs. This inference is not sourced this session.
- Using a macOS privileged helper (SMAppService) just to read power adds review and notarization complexity. An estimated-watts model is the pragmatic choice for macOS.

### Gaps
- No electricity-price API was researched. Candidates include EIA (US), ENTSO-E/Eurostat (EU) and Octopus Agile-type dynamic tariffs. No source found on how Kryptex or NiceHash compute their "net profit" displays.

## 5. Legal, tax and compliance (US + EU) and app store policies

### Takeaway
The least risky structure is for users to be paid directly by the backend (NiceHash, Kryptex Pool, Salad and others) into their own accounts or wallets, with the app as software only. Pooling and redistributing earnings, especially in crypto, raises money-transmission (US FinCEN/state) and MiCA CASP questions. FinCEN's 2019 guidance suggests an "integral" exemption for passing on mining payouts, but flags operators that host members' wallets. On distribution: the Mac App Store and Microsoft Store both ban on-device mining apps, so direct download is required for mining modules.

### Cited Findings
**US money transmission**
- FinCEN FIN-2019-G001 (9 May 2019) consolidates how MSB rules apply to convertible virtual currency (CVC) business models. It imposes no new requirements — [Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2019/05/applying-the-bank-secrecy-act-framework); [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2019/05/fincen-issues-guidance-on-convertible-virtual-currencies/)
- Exemption reading: people "passing on payments for offering computing power in mining activities" fall under the integral exemption, provided they offer no ancillary services — [Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2019/05/applying-the-bank-secrecy-act-framework). ABA also lists mining pools and cloud miners as possibly exempt — [ABA](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2019/05/fincen-issues-guidance-on-convertible-virtual-currencies/)
- Risk reading: mining pool operators "who host all the wallets on behalf of pool members" appear to need MSB registration — [Hunton/Mondaq summary](https://huntonak.com/blockchain-legal-resource/new-fincen-guidance-on-virtual-currency). The two readings depend on custody and ancillary-service facts. The guidance is from 2019 and later FinCEN items were not checked.
- Market pattern: Salad pays in non-cash "reward points/balance" redeemable for gift cards or PayPal via a storefront — [Salad Terms for Chefs](https://salad.com/terms/chefs/). Honeygain's JumpTask moved crypto payouts to a third-party payout partner (GIFQ) and fixed-value credits in Aug 2026 — [Honeygain JumpTask 2026](https://support.honeygain.com/hc/en-us/articles/28445831396380-What-s-changing-in-JumpTask-2026). Kryptex uses a partner exchange and Volet for fiat — [Kryptex Fees](https://www.kryptex.com/en/fees)

**US tax reporting**
- Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, the 1099-NEC/1099-MISC threshold rises from $600 to $2,000 for payments made after 31 Dec 2025, with inflation indexing from 2027. The backup-withholding threshold also rises to $2,000 — [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-1099-reporting-threshold.html); [OnPay](https://onpay.com/insights/1099-reporting-threshold-updates/)
- 1099-K threshold for third-party settlement organizations is restored to over $20,000 and over 200 transactions, retroactively. Some states keep $600 thresholds — [TaxAct](https://blog.taxact.com/new-form-1099-k-reporting-thresholds/); [1099online](https://www.1099online.com/blog/form-1099-k-threshold/)
- Income below the thresholds is still taxable. W-9 collection is still advisable — [OnPay](https://onpay.com/insights/1099-reporting-threshold-updates/)

**EU**
- DAC8 has applied since 1 Jan 2026. Crypto-asset service providers must collect user identity, tax residence and TIN, and report transactions annually. The first exchange between authorities, covering 2026, is due by 30 Sep 2027 — [RSM](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/dac8-and-carf-present-extensive-reporting-challenges-for-crypto-platforms.html); [Deloitte Malta](https://www.deloitte.com/mt/en/services/tax/perspectives/tax-alerts/Gearing-up-for-crypto-asset-tax-reporting-requirements-in-2026--.html); [CoinGeek](https://coingeek.com/eu-kicks-off-2026-with-new-digital-asset-tax-reporting-rules/)
- MiCA's transitional period ended on 1 July 2026. Only authorized CASPs may now provide crypto-asset services in the EU, and custody and transfer services are regulated — [Elvinger Hoss](https://elvingerhoss.lu/insights/publications/mica-transitional-period-has-come-end-what-comes-next-casps); [ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/215793); [MFSA Dear CEO letter June 2026](https://www.mfsa.mt/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Dear-CEO-Letter-VFA-Licence-and-Transition-to-CASP-under-the-Markets-in-Crypto-Assets-Act-MiCA-Act.pdf). Protocol-level or solo staking without an intermediary is described as not explicitly in scope, which by analogy may apply to solo mining — [Everstake](https://everstake.one/resources/blog/staking-mica-what-eu-institutions-need-to-know-in-2026). A Commission consultation on a MiCA review closed on 31 Aug 2026 — [ESMA/Elvinger Hoss](https://elvingerhoss.lu/insights/publications/mica-transitional-period-has-come-end-what-comes-next-casps)

**App store policies**
- Apple guideline 2.4.2: apps, including their third-party ads, "may not run unrelated background processes, such as cryptocurrency mining". Guideline 3.1.5(b)(ii): apps may not mine unless processing is off-device (cloud mining). This was introduced in June 2018 after the "Calendar 2" Mac app mined in exchange for pro features — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/apple/apple-bans-apps-that-mine-cryptocurrencies); [Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/apple-app-store-review-guidelines-mining); [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2018/06/12/crytocurrency-mining-ios-apps/). These are 2018 sources; the current wording should be checked on developer.apple.com.
- Microsoft Store policy 10.2.6: "Apps that enable the mining of crypto-currency on device are not allowed". Apps for remote management of mining, wallets and trading are allowed. It was revised in Oct 2024 (policy v7.18) — [Microsoft Store Policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/windows/uwp/publish/store-policies); [Store policy change history](https://docs.microsoft.com:443/en-us/windows/uwp/publish/store-policies-change-history)
- Microsoft also bans mining on its online services without written approval, so a CI build or test farm can't mine on Azure — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/microsoft-bans-cryptomining-in-azure); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/195314/microsoft-prohibits-crypto-mining-on-online-services-without-permission)

### Inferences
- Architecture with the legal question in mind: make the app non-custodial. Each backend pays the user directly (the user's NiceHash, Salad or Vast account, or the user's own wallet address for pools). The app makes money through (a) affiliate/referral rev-share from backends, (b) a disclosed developer fee taken as a slice of hashing time ("devfee", as many miners do), or (c) a premium subscription. This avoids the app holding user funds, which is the trigger for FinCEN MSB, state MTL and MiCA CASP scrutiny. It also avoids 1099/DAC8 issuer duties falling on the app.
- If a single unified balance in fiat or gift cards is still wanted, copy Salad and JumpTask: non-cash reward points plus a licensed payout partner (Tremendous, GIFQ, PayPal Payouts) that does the KYC. Get counsel's sign-off, because gift-card and prepaid-access rules (FinCEN prepaid access, state escheat) apply.
- Store distribution: a "lite" Microsoft Store edition could offer only remote management, monitoring and AI-rental modules if those count as allowed. The miner module must be a direct download. On the Mac App Store, any on-device compute-for-pay is likely to be rejected under 2.4.2/3.1.5 even for non-mining AI workloads, because 2.4.2 bans "unrelated background processes" generally. Ship macOS outside the store as a notarized Developer ID app.

### Gaps
- FinCEN guidance after 2019 (2023–2026 actions on mixers or custodial services) and US state money-transmitter rules for gift-card or points-based payouts were not researched.
- No direct source on whether MiCA treats a pool or aggregator that redistributes mining rewards as a CASP.
- How IRS treats mining or compute-rental income (ordinary income at fair market value on receipt), and whether a company paying users in crypto must issue 1099-MISC or 1099-DA, was not confirmed from IRS sources this session.
- The current text of Apple guidelines 2.4.2 and 3.1.5 (2026 revision) was not fetched directly.

## 6. Security risks of running third-party workloads on consumer PCs (and how to communicate them)

### Takeaway
The real risks are untrusted code (mitigated by VM or container isolation such as WSL2/Hyper-V), weakened AV posture (vendors telling users to disable PUA protection), anti-cheat and VPN conflicts, hardware wear and heat, and impersonation by malware bundling miners. Existing vendors handle this with "it's a false positive" FAQs, which builds little trust.

### Cited Findings
- Isolation claim: Salad says WSL2 sandboxing protects the PC from container workloads — [Salad community](https://community.salad.com/container-workloads-and-your-pc/)
- Miners are flagged as PUA because threat actors often bundle them silently with malware, so a legitimate miner looks like cryptojacking to AV. Source quality is low; the observation is consistent with Microsoft's PUA category — [malware families explorer](https://jordan123234-malware-families-explorer.static.hf.space/families/nicehashminer.html); [Microsoft Answers](https://answers.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/forum/all/windows-defender-puawin32coinminer-active-false/e2f3213a-a8f6-4a0d-b357-9097bff65548)
- Kryptex tells users to disable PUA blocking. Salad tells users to whitelist the app — [Kryptex](https://www.kryptex.com/de/articles/not-a-virus-en); [Salad](https://support.salad.com/article/205-is-salad-a-virus)
- Reviewers raise hardware wear and electricity concerns — [Trustpilot Salad](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/salad.com)
- Scam plug-ins impersonating earn-network clients circulate (Nodepay "1.5× income" backdoor plug-in) — [PANews](https://www.panewslab.com/en/articles/afahxv56gfqm)
- RAPL energy counters are a known side channel (PLATYPUS), which is why Linux restricts them. Loosening permissions for metering slightly increases attack surface — [Intel INTEL-SA-00389](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/software-security-guidance/advisory-guidance/running-average-power-limit-energy-reporting.html); [CodeCarbon](https://docs.codecarbon.io/latest/how-to/enable-rapl/)

### Inferences
- How to communicate risk: give a plain-language "What runs on your PC" panel per backend (container vs native binary, network access, disk use). Use narrow, consent-based folder exclusions only, never global AV disablement. Publish binary hashes and signatures for every downloaded engine. Default to a GPU temperature cap and power limit (NVML). Pause automatically when games or fullscreen apps run. Warn about anti-cheat and BIOS virtualization up front. Show a one-click uninstall that removes exclusions, WSL distros and scheduled tasks.
- Native miners run with user privileges and are not isolated. Only container or VM-based AI workloads get a real sandbox. The UI should state that difference honestly.

### Gaps
- No documented 2025–2026 security incidents (escapes, data theft) involving Salad, Vast or other consumer compute hosts were found in this session. No source on WSL2/Hyper-V escape risk for GPU-paravirtualized containers.
